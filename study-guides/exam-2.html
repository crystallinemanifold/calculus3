  1. 01Chapter 4
    Given \( f(x, y) =\displaystyle \frac{\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}}{x + 3} \):
    1. Sketch the domain of \( f \).
    2. Calculate \( f(3, 1) \) and \( f(-5, -3) \).
    3. Find a point satisfying \( f(x, y) = 1 \).
    Solution to exercise 1
    • (a) \( f \) is defined where \( x^2 - y^2 \geq 0 \) and \( x + 3 \neq 0 \). We solve these two inequalities: \[\begin{aligned} x^2 - y^2 &\geq 0 \Rightarrow x^2 \geq y^2 \Rightarrow |x| \geq |y| \\x + 3 &\neq 0 \Rightarrow x \neq -3 \end{aligned}\] Therefore, the domain of \( f \) is the following set: \[ D = \{ (x, y) : |x| \geq |y|, x \neq -3 \} \]
    • (b) To find \( f(3, 1) \) we substitute \( x = 3 \), \( y = 1 \) in \( f(x, y) \). We get \[ f(3, 1) = \frac{\sqrt{3^2 - 1^2}}{3 + 3} = \frac{\sqrt{8}}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3} \] Similarly, setting \( x = -5 \), \( y = -3 \), we get \[ f(-5, -3) = \frac{\sqrt{(-5)^2 - (-3)^2}}{-5 + 3} = \frac{\sqrt{16}}{-2} = -2. \]
    • (c) We must find a point \( (x, y) \) such that \[ f(x, y) = \frac{\sqrt{x^2 - y^2}}{x + 3} = 1 \] We choose, for instance, \( y = 1 \), substitute and solve for \( x \). This gives \[ \frac{\sqrt{x^2 - 1^2}}{x + 3} = 1 \] \[ \sqrt{x^2 - 1} = x + 3 \] \[ x^2 - 1 = (x + 3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9 \] \[ 6x = -10 \Rightarrow x = -\frac{5}{3} \] Thus, the point \( \left(-\frac{5}{3}, 1\right) \) satisfies \( f\left(-\frac{5}{3}, 1\right) = 1 \).

  6. 06Chapter 4
    Referring to the contour map in Figure 2:
    1. Estimate the average rate of change of elevation from \( A \) to \( B \) and from \( A \) to \( D \).
    2. Estimate the directional derivative at \( A \) in the direction of \( \mathbf{v} \).
    3. What are the signs of \( f_x \) and \( f_y \) at \( D \)?
    4. At which of the labeled points are both \( f_x \) and \( f_y \) negative?
    Solution to exercise 6
    (a) From \( A \) to \( B \): The segment \( \overline{AB} \) spans 6 level curves and the contour interval is \( m = 50 \, \text{m} \), so the change of altitude is \( 6 \cdot 50 = 300 \, \text{m} \). From the horizontal scale of the contour map we see that the horizontal distance from \( A \) to \( B \) is 2 km or 2000 m. Therefore, \[ \text{Average ROC from } A \text{ to } B = \frac{\Delta \text{altitude}}{\Delta \text{horizontal distance}} = \frac{300}{2000} = 0.15 \] From \( A \) to \( D \): \( A \) and \( D \) lie on the same level curve, hence there is no change in altitude from \( A \) to \( D \). Therefore, \[ \text{Average ROC from } A \text{ to } D = \frac{0}{\Delta \text{horizontal distance}} = 0. \] (b) We first estimate the gradient at \( A \). We get \[ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \bigg|_A \approx \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} \approx \frac{0}{\Delta x} = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \nabla f_A \approx (0, 0.25) \] \[ \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \bigg|_A \approx \frac{\Delta f}{\Delta y} \approx \frac{50}{200} \approx 0.25 \] We estimate \( \mathbf{v} \) by \( \mathbf{v} \approx \left( \frac{4}{9}, 1 \right) \approx (0.44, 1) \), hence the cosine of the angle between \( \mathbf{v} \) and the gradient at \( A \) is \[ \cos \theta = \frac{\langle (0, 0.25) \cdot (0.44, 1) \rangle}{0.25 \cdot \sqrt{0.44^2 + 1}} = \frac{0.25}{0.25 \cdot 1.093} = 0.915 \] Hence, \[ D_{\mathbf{v}} f(A) = \|\nabla f_A \| \cos \theta = 0.25 \cdot 0.915 \approx 0.229. \] (Another method is to note that in the direction of \( \mathbf{v} \), we cross four contour lines in about 1000 meters; thus, the change of \( f \) in that direction is about \( 4 \cdot 50 / 1000 = 0.2 \).) (c) At \( D \), as we move east we are going downhill, towards lower contours, so that \( f_x \) is negative. As we move north, we are going uphill, towards higher contours, so that \( f_y \) is positive. (d) At \( A \), moving north is uphill, so that \( f_y \) is positive. At \( B \), moving east is uphill, so that \( f_x \) is positive. But at \( C \), moving east or moving north heads from the contour at 700 to the contour at 650, so that both \( f_x \) and \( f_y \) are negative.
  7. 07Chapter 4
    Describe the level curves of:
    1. \(\displaystyle f(x, y) = e^{4x-y} \)
    2. \( f(x, y) = \ln(4x - y) \)
    3. \( f(x, y) = 3x^2 - 4y^2 \)
    4. \( f(x, y) = x + y^2 \)
    Solution to exercise 7
    (a) The level curves of \( f(x, y) = e^{4x - y} \) are the curves \( e^{4x - y} = c \) in the \( xy \)-plane, where \( c > 0 \). Taking \( \ln \) from both sides, we get \( 4x - y = \ln c \). Therefore, the level curves are the parallel lines of slope 4, \( 4x - y = \ln c \), \( c > 0 \), in the \( xy \)-plane. (b) The level curves of \( f(x, y) = \ln(4x - y) \) are the curves \( \ln(4x - y) = c \) in the \( xy \)-plane. We rewrite it as \( 4x - y = e^c \) to obtain the parallel lines of slope 4, with negative \( y \)-intercepts. (c) The level curves of \( f(x, y) = 3x^2 - 4y^2 \) are the hyperbolas \( 3x^2 - 4y^2 = c \) in the \( xy \)-plane. (d) The level curves of \( f(x, y) = x + y^2 \) are the curves \( x + y^2 = c \) or \( x = c - y^2 \) in the \( xy \)-plane. These are parabolas whose axis is the \( x \)-axis.

    For \(c=0\), the third family degenerates to the two lines \(y=\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}x\).

    Solution to exercise 9
    The function \( f(x, y) = xy + y^2 \) is continuous everywhere because it is a polynomial, therefore we evaluate the limit using substitution: \[ \lim_{(x, y) \to (1, -3)} \left( xy + y^2 \right) = 1 \cdot (-3) + (-3)^2 = 6 \]
  2. 10Chapter 4
    \( \displaystyle \lim_{(x,y) \to (1,-3)} \ln(3x + y)\)
    Solution to exercise 10
    Approaching \( (1, -3) \) along the ray \( y = -3 \), \( x > 1 \) gives \[ \lim_{x \to 1^+} \ln(3x - 3) = -\infty \] Therefore, \( f \) takes on arbitrarily small values at the intersection of every disk around the point \( (1, -3) \) with the domain of the function. This shows that \[ \lim_{(x, y) \to (1, -3)} \ln(3x + y) \text{ does not exist.} \]

    There is no finite real limit. In the extended-real sense, the limit is \(-\infty\): within the domain, \(3x+y\to0^+\).

  3. 11Chapter 4
    \( \displaystyle \lim_{(x,y) \to (0,0)} \frac{xy + xy^2}{x^2 + y^2}\)
    Solution to exercise 11
    We evaluate the limits as \( (x, y) \) approaches the origin along the lines \( y = x \) and \( y = 2x \): \[ \lim_{\substack{(x, y) \to (0, 0)\\\text{ along } y = x}} \frac{xy + xy^2}{x^2 + y^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x \cdot x + x \cdot x^2}{x^2 + x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2 + x^3}{2x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 + x}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \] \[ \lim_{\substack{x \to 0, (0, 0)\\\text{ along } y = 2x}} \frac{xy + xy^2}{x^2 + y^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x \cdot 2x + x \cdot (2x)^2}{x^2 + (2x)^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2x^2 + 4x^3}{5x^2} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2 + 4x}{5} = \frac{2}{5} \] Since the two limits are different, \( f(x, y) \) does not approach one limit as \( (x, y) \to (0, 0) \), therefore the limit does not exist.

    Solution to exercise 14
    We have \[ \lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 2)} \frac{(e^x - 1)(e^y - 1)}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x} \lim_{y \to 2} (e^y - 1) = \left( e^2 - 1 \right) \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x} \tag{1}\label{exam2-equation-1} \] By L'Hôpital's Rule, \[ \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x - 1}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{d}{dx}(e^x - 1)}{\frac{d}{dx}(x)} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{e^x}{1} = 1 \tag{2}\label{exam2-equation-2} \] Combining (1) and (2) we conclude that \[ \lim_{(x, y) \to (0, 2)} \frac{(e^x - 1)(e^y - 1)}{x} = \left( e^2 - 1 \right) \cdot 1 = e^2 - 1. \]

  1. 16Chapter 4
    Calculate \( f_x(1, 3) \) and \( f_y(1, 3) \) for \( f(x, y) = \sqrt{7x + y^2} \).
    Solution to exercise 16
    To calculate \( f_x(x, y) \) we treat \( y \) as a constant and use the Chain Rule. This gives \[ f_x(x, y) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \sqrt{7x + y^2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{7x + y^2}} \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(7x + y^2) = \frac{7}{2\sqrt{7x + y^2}} \] We compute \( f_y(x, y) \) similarly, treating \( x \) as a constant: \[ f_y(x, y) = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \sqrt{7x + y^2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{7x + y^2}} \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(7x + y^2) = \frac{2y}{2\sqrt{7x + y^2}} = \frac{y}{\sqrt{7x + y^2}} \] At the point \( (1, 3) \) we have \[ f_x(1, 3) = \frac{7}{2\sqrt{7 \cdot 1 + 3^2}} = \frac{7}{2 \cdot 4} = \frac{7}{8} \] \[ f_y(1, 3) = \frac{3}{\sqrt{7 \cdot 1 + 3^2}} = \frac{3}{4} \]

  1. 17Chapter 4
    \( f(x, y) = 2x + y^2 \)
    Solution to exercise 17
    To find \( f_x \) we treat \( y \) as a constant, and to find \( f_y \) we treat \( x \) as a constant. We get \[ f_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( 2x + y^2 \right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( 2x \right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( y^2 \right) = 2 + 0 = 2 \] \[ f_y = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( 2x + y^2 \right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( 2x \right) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( y^2 \right) = 0 + 2y = 2y \]
  2. 18Chapter 4
    \( f(x, y) = 4xy^3 \)
    Solution to exercise 18
    We compute \( f_x \), treating \( y \) as a constant: \[ f_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} (4xy^3) = 4y^3 \frac{\partial}{\partial x} (x) = 4y^3 \cdot 1 = 4y^3 \] We compute \( f_y \), treating \( x \) as a constant: \[ f_y = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} (4xy^3) = 4x \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial y} (y^3) = 4x \cdot 3y^2 = 12xy^2 \]
  3. 19Chapter 4
    \( f(x, y) = \sin(xy)e^{-x-y} \)
    Solution to exercise 19
    We compute \( f_x \), treating \( y \) as a constant and using the Product Rule and the Chain Rule. We get \[ f_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \sin(xy) e^{-x-y} \right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \sin(xy) \right) e^{-x-y} + \sin(xy) \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( e^{-x-y} \right) \] \[ = \cos(xy) \cdot y e^{-x-y} + \sin(xy) \cdot (-1) e^{-x-y} = e^{-x-y} \left( y \cos(xy) - \sin(xy) \right) \] We compute \( f_y \) similarly, treating \( x \) as a constant. Notice that since \( f(y, x) = f(x, y) \), the partial derivative \( f_y \) can be obtained from \( f_x \) by interchanging \( x \) and \( y \). That is, \[ f_y = e^{-x-y} \left( x \cos(xy) - \sin(xy) \right). \]
  4. 20Chapter 4
    \( f(x, y) = \ln(x^2 + xy^2) \)
    Solution to exercise 20
    Using the Chain Rule we obtain \[ f_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \ln \left( x^2 + xy^2 \right) = \frac{1}{x^2 + xy^2} \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( x^2 + xy^2 \right) = \frac{1}{x^2 + xy^2} \cdot \left( 2x + y^2 \right) = \frac{2x + y^2}{x^2 + xy^2} \] \[ f_y = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \ln \left( x^2 + xy^2 \right) = \frac{1}{x^2 + xy^2} \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( x^2 + xy^2 \right) = \frac{1}{x^2 + xy^2} \cdot \left( 2xy \right) = \frac{2xy}{x^2 + xy^2} \]
  5. 21Chapter 4
    Calculate \( f_{xxyz} \) for \( f(x, y, z) = y \sin(x + z) \).
    Solution to exercise 21
    We differentiate \( f \) twice with respect to \( x \), once with respect to \( y \), and finally with respect to \( z \). This gives \[ f_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( y \sin(x + z) \right) = y \cos(x + z) \] \[ f_{xx} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( y \cos(x + z) \right) = -y \sin(x + z) \] \[ f_{xxy} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y} \left( -y \sin(x + z) \right) = -\sin(x + z) \] \[ f_{xxyz} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \left( -\sin(x + z) \right) = -\cos(x + z) \]
  6. 22Chapter 4
    Fix \( c > 0 \). Show that for any constants \( \alpha, \beta \), the function \( u(t, x) = \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \sin(\alpha x) \) satisfies the wave equation \[ \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} \]
    Solution to exercise 22
    We compute the partial derivatives \( u_t \) and \( u_x \) using the Chain Rule: \[ u_t = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \sin(\alpha x) \right) = \sin(\alpha x) \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \right) = \sin(\alpha x) \cos(\alpha ct + \beta) \cdot \alpha c \] \[ u_x = \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \sin(\alpha x) \right) = \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \sin(\alpha x) \right) = \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \cos(\alpha x) \cdot \alpha \] We find \( u_{tt} \) and \( u_{xx} \), differentiating \( u_t \) and \( u_x \) with respect to \( t \) and \( x \), respectively: \[ u_{tt} = \alpha c \sin(\alpha x) \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \left( \cos(\alpha ct + \beta) \right) = -\alpha^2 c^2 \sin(\alpha x) \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \] \[ u_{xx} = \alpha \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \left( \cos(\alpha x) \right) = -\alpha^2 \sin(\alpha ct + \beta) \sin(\alpha x) \] We see that \( u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx} \).

  2. 24Chapter 4
    Suppose that \( f(4, 4) = 3 \) and \( f_x(4, 4) = f_y(4, 4) = -1 \). Use the linear approximation to estimate \( f(4.1, 4) \) and \( f(3.88, 4.03) \).
    Solution to exercise 24
    The linear approximation is \[ f(a + h, b + k) \approx f(a, b) + f_x(a, b)h + f_y(a, b)k \] We use the linear approximation at the point \( (4, 4) \). Therefore, estimating \( f(3.88, 4.03) \), \[ h = 3.88 - 4 = -0.12 \quad \text{and} \quad k = 4.03 - 4 = 0.03 \] \[ f(3.88, 4.03) \approx f(4, 4) + f_x(4, 4)(-0.12) + f_y(4, 4) \cdot 0.03 \] \[ f(3.88, 4.03) \approx 3 - 1 \cdot (-0.12) - 1 \cdot 0.03 = 3.09 \] Estimating \( f(4.1, 4) \), \[ h = 4.1 - 4 = 0.1 \quad \text{and} \quad k = 4 - 4 = 0 \] \[ f(4.1, 4) \approx f(4, 4) + f_x(4, 4)(0.1) + f_y(4, 4) \cdot 0 \] \[ f(4.1, 4) \approx 3 - 1 \cdot (0.1) - 1 \cdot 0 = 2.9 \] We obtain the estimations \( f(3.88, 4.03) \approx 3.09 \) and \( f(4.1, 4) \approx 2.9 \).
  3. 25Chapter 4
    Use a linear approximation of \( f(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z} \) to estimate \( \sqrt{7.1^2 + 4.9^2 + 69.5} \). Compare with a calculator value.
    Solution to exercise 25
    The function whose value we want to approximate is \[ f(x, y, z) = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z} \] We will use the linear approximation at the point \( (7, 5, 70) \).     Use Lagrange multipliers to find the dimensions of a cylindrical can with a bottom but no top, of fixed volume \( V \) with minimum surface area.
    Solution to exercise 61
    We denote the radius of the cylinder by \( r \) and the height by \( h \).

    The volume of the cylinder is \(\pi r^2 h\) and the surface area is \(f = 2\pi r h + \pi r^2\). We need to minimize \( f(r, h) = 2\pi r h + \pi r^2 \) subject to the constraint \( g(r, h) = \pi r^2 h - V = 0 \). Step 1. Write out the Lagrange Equations. We have \( \nabla f = (2\pi h + 2\pi r, 2\pi r) \) and \( \nabla g = \pi \langle 2hr, r^2 \rangle \), hence the Lagrange Condition \( \nabla f = \lambda \nabla g \) is \[ \pi \langle 2h + 2r, 2r \rangle = \pi \lambda \langle 2hr, r^2 \rangle \] or \[ \langle 2h + 2r, 2r \rangle = \lambda \langle 2hr, r^2 \rangle \] We obtain the following equations: \[ 2(h + r) = 2hr \lambda \quad \Rightarrow \quad h + r = hr \lambda \] \[ 2r = \lambda r^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 2r = \lambda r^2 \] Step 2. Solve for \( \lambda \) in terms of \( r \) and \( h \). The equation of the constraint implies that \( r \neq 0 \) and \( h \neq 0 \) (we assume that \( V > 0 \)).
