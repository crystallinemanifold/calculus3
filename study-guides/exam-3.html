Riemann sums & symmetry

  1. 01Chapter 5

    Let \(S_{M,N}\) be the double Riemann sum for \(\displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} f(x,y) \, dA\) with \(M\) subdivisions of the \(x\)-interval and \(N\) subdivisions of the \(y\)-interval. Calculate the Riemann sum \(S_{2,3}\) for \(\displaystyle \int_{1}^{4} \int_{2}^{6} x^2y \, dx \, dy\) using two choices of sample points:

    1. Lower-left vertex

    2. Midpoint of rectangle

    Then calculate the exact value of the double integral.

    Solution to exercise 1

    Divide \([2,6]\times[1,4]\) into two columns and three rows. Then \(\Delta A=2\).

    (a) Lower-left sample points have \(x=2,4\), \(y=1,2,3\):

    \[ S_{2,3}=2(2^2+4^2)(1+2+3)=240. \]

    (b) Midpoints have \(x=3,5\), \(y=3/2,5/2,7/2\):

    \[ S_{2,3}=2(3^2+5^2)\left(\frac32+\frac52+\frac72\right)=510. \]

    The exact integral is

    \[ \left(\int_2^6x^2\,dx\right)\left(\int_1^4y\,dy\right)=\frac{208}{3}\cdot\frac{15}{2}=520. \]
  2. 04Chapter 5

    Explain the following:

    1. \(\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-1}^{1} \sin(xy) \, dx \, dy = 0\)

    2. \(\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-1}^{1} \cos(xy) \, dx \, dy > 0\)

    Solution to exercise 4

    (a) The double integral is the signed volume of the region between the graph of \( f(x, y) \) and the \( xy \)-plane.

    The function \( f(x, y) = \sin(xy) \) satisfies \( f(-x, y) = \sin(-xy) = -\sin(xy) = -f(x, y) \), hence the region left of the y-axis cancels with the region to the right of the y-axis. Therefore the double integral is zero.

    (b) The function \( f(x, y) = \cos(xy) \) satisfies \( f(-x, y) = f(x, -y) = f(-x, -y) = f(x, y) \), hence

    \[\int_{-1}^{1} \int_{-1}^{1} \cos(xy) \,dx \,dy = 4 \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \cos(xy) \,dx \,dy.\]

    Since \( 0 \leq xy \leq 1 \) for the square \([0, 1] \times [0, 1]\), we have \(\cos(xy) > 0\), and the double integral is positive.

    2. 06Chapter 5

      \(\displaystyle \int_{1/2}^{0} \int_{0}^{\pi/6} e^{2y} \sin(3x) \, dx \, dy\)

      Solution to exercise 6

      We use an iterated integral of a product function to compute the double integral as the product of two single integrals. That is,

      \[\int_{1/2}^{0} \int_{0}^{\pi/6} e^{2y} \sin 3x \,dx \,dy = \left( \int_{1/2}^{0} e^{2y} \,dy \right) \left( \int_{0}^{\pi/6} \sin 3x \,dx \right)\]
      \[= \left( \frac{1}{2} \left[ e^{2y} \right]_{1/2}^{0} \right) \left( -\frac{1}{3} \left[ \cos 3x \right]_{0}^{\pi/6} \right)\]
      \[= \frac{1}{2} (1 - e) \cdot \left( -\frac{1}{3} \right) ( \cos \frac{\pi}{2} - \cos 0 ) = \frac{1}{6} (1 - e)\]
    3. 07Chapter 5

      \(\displaystyle \int_{0}^{\pi/3} \int_{0}^{\pi/6} \sin(x + y) \, dx \, dy\)

      Solution to exercise 7

      We compute the inner integral treating \(y\) as a constant:

      \[\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \sin(x + y) \,dx = -\cos(x + y) \Bigg|_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} = -\cos \left( \frac{\pi}{6} + y \right) + \cos y = \cos y - \cos \left( \frac{\pi}{6} + y \right)\]
      We now integrate the result with respect to \(y\):
      \[\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6}} \sin(x + y) \,dx \,dy = \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{3}} \left( \cos y - \cos \left( y + \frac{\pi}{6} \right) \right) \,dy = \sin y - \sin \left( y + \frac{\pi}{6} \right) \Bigg|_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{3}}\]
      \[= \sin \frac{\pi}{3} - \sin \left( \frac{\pi}{3} + \frac{\pi}{6} \right) - \left( \sin 0 - \sin \frac{\pi}{6} \right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} - 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2}\]

      2. 16Chapter 5

        Let \(W\) be the region bounded by the planes \(y = z\), \(z + 2y = 3\), and \(z = 0\) for \(0 \leq x \leq 2\).

        1. Express the triple integral \(\displaystyle \iiint\limits_W f(x, y, z) \, dV\) as an iterated integral in the order \(dy \, dx \, dz\) (project \(W\) onto the \(xz\)-plane).

        2. Evaluate the triple integral for \(f(x, y, z) =1\).

        3. Compute the volume of \(W\) using geometry and check that the result coincides with the answer to (b).

        Solution to exercise 16

        The region \(W\) is is the prism shown in the figure:

        (a) The projection of \( W \) onto the \(xz\)-plane is the rectangle \( D \) given by

        \[0 \leq x \leq 2, \quad 0 \leq z \leq 1.\]
        For each point of \( D \), the range of \( y \) values is from \( z \) to \( \frac{3-z}{2} \), so that the integral is
        \[I = \iiint_W f \,dV = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{z}^{\frac{3-z}{2}} f(x, y, z) \,dy \,dx \,dz.\]
        (b) When \( f(x, y, z) = 1 \) we get
        \[\int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{z}^{(3-z)/2} 1 \,dy \,dx \,dz = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} y\bigg|_{z}^{(3-z)/2} \,dx \,dz\]
        \[= \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \left( \frac{3 - 3z}{2} \right) \,dx \,dz\]
        \[= \frac{3}{2} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} (1 - z) \,dx \,dz\]
        \[= \frac{3}{2} \int_{0}^{1} x(1 - z)\bigg|_{0}^{2} \,dz\]
        \[= \frac{3}{2} \int_{0}^{1} 2(1 - z) \,dz\]
        \[= 3\left[z-\frac12z^2\right]_0^1 = \frac{3}{2}.\]

        (c) To determine the answer the answer geometrically, note that this prism sits over a triangular base in the \(yz\)-plane:

        The line \( z = 3 - 2y \) intersects the \(y\)-axis at \( y = \frac{3}{2} \), so the triangle has base \( \frac{3}{2} \) and altitude \( \tau = 1 \), so its area is \( \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot 1 = \frac{3}{4} \). Since the height of the prism is 2, the volume of the prism is \( \frac{3}{4} \cdot 2 = \frac{3}{2} \), which agrees with the answer in part (b).

      3. 17Chapter 5

        Let \(D\) be the domain between \(y = x\) and \(y = \sqrt{x}\). Calculate \(\displaystyle \iint\limits_D xy \, dA\) as an iterated integral in the order \(dx \, dy\) and \(dy \, dx\).

        Solution to exercise 17

        In the order \( dx \, dy \): The inequalities describing \( D \) as a horizontally simple region are obtained by first rewriting the equations of the curves with \( x \) as a function of \( y \), that is, \( x = y \) and \( x = y^2 \), respectively. The points of intersection are found solving the equation

        \[y = y^2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad y(1 - y) = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad y = 0, \, y = 1\]
        We obtain the following inequalities for \( D \) (see figure):
        \[D: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \quad y^2 \leq x \leq y\]

        We now compute the double integral as the following iterated integral:

        \[\iint_D xy \,dA = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{y^2}^{y} xy \,dx \,dy = \int_{0}^{1} \left[ \frac{x^2y}{2} \right]_{x=y^2}^{x=y} \,dy = \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{y \cdot y^2}{2} - \frac{y^4 \cdot y}{2} \right) \,dy\]
        \[= \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{y^3}{2} - \frac{y^5}{2} \right) \,dy = \left[ \frac{y^4}{8} - \frac{y^6}{12} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{24}\]
        In the order \( dy \, dx \): \( D \) is described as a vertically simple region by the following inequalities (see figure):
        \[D : 0 \leq x \leq 1, \quad x \leq y \leq \sqrt{x}\]

        The corresponding iterated integral is

        \[\iint_D xy \,dA = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{x}^{\sqrt{x}} xy \,dy \,dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left[ \frac{xy^2}{2} \right]_{y=x}^{y=\sqrt{x}} \,dx\]
        \[= \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{x \cdot x}{2} - \frac{x \cdot x^2}{2} \right) \,dx = \int_{0}^{1} \left( \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{2} \right) \,dx\]
        \[= \left[ \frac{x^3}{6} - \frac{x^4}{8} \right]_{0}^{1} = \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{24}\]

      3. 25Chapter 5

        Find the volume of the region between the graph of the function \(f(x, y) = 1 - (x^2 + y^2)\) and the \(xy\)-plane.

        Solution to exercise 25

        SOLUTION.

        The intersection of the surface \(z = 1 - (x^2 + y^2)\) with the \(xy\)-plane is obtained by setting \(z = 0\). That is,

        \[1 - (x^2 + y^2) = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x^2 + y^2 = 1.\]
        Therefore, the projection of the solid onto the \(xy\)-plane is the disk \(x^2 + y^2 \leq 1\). We describe the disk as a vertically simple region:
        \[\mathcal{D} : -1 \leq x \leq 1, \quad -\sqrt{1-x^2} \leq y \leq \sqrt{1-x^2}.\]

        \[= 2\pi \int_0^1 \big(r - r^3\big) \, dr = \frac{\pi}{2}.\]

      3. 28Chapter 5

        Calculate \(\displaystyle \iiint\limits_{W} xyz \, dV\), where \(W=\{0 \leq x \leq 1, x \leq y \leq 1, x \leq z \leq x+y \}\).

        Solution to exercise 28

        \(\mathcal{W}\) is the region between the two planes \(z = x\) and \(z = x + y\) lying over the triangle \(\mathcal{D}\), defined by \(0 \leq x \leq 1, \, x \leq y \leq 1\). Therefore, the triple integral is equal to the following iterated integral:

        \[= \int_0^1 \int_x^1 \frac{xy}{2} \big((x+y)^2 - x^2\big) \, dy \, dx\]

      6. 31Chapter 5

        Find the volume of the solid contained in the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\) below the surface \(z = (x + y)^2\) and above the surface \(z = -(x - y)^2\).

        Solution to exercise 31

        We rewrite the equations of the surfaces using cylindrical coordinates:

        \[z = (x + y)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + 2xy = r^2 + 2r \cos\theta (r \sin\theta) = r^2 (1 + \sin 2\theta)\]
        \[z = -(x - y)^2 = -(x^2 + y^2 - 2xy) = -(r^2 - 2r^2 \cos\theta \sin\theta) = -r^2 (1 - \sin 2\theta)\]

        The projection of the solid onto the \( xy \)-plane is the unit disk. Therefore, the solid is described by the following inequalities:

        \[W : 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \quad 0 \leq r \leq 1, \quad -r^2 (1 - \sin 2\theta) \leq z \leq r^2 (1 + \sin 2\theta)\]

        Expressing the volume as a triple integral and converting the triple integral to cylindrical coordinates, we get

        \[V = \text{Volume}(W) = \iiint_W 1 \, dV = \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 \int_{-r^2(1-\sin 2\theta)}^{r^2(1+\sin 2\theta)} 1 \, dz \, r \, dr \, d\theta\]
        \[= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 \left[ r z \right]_{z=-r^2(1-\sin 2\theta)}^{z=r^2(1+\sin 2\theta)} dr \, d\theta\]
        \[= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 r \left( r^2(1+\sin 2\theta) + r^2(1-\sin 2\theta) \right) dr \, d\theta\]
        \[= \int_0^{2\pi} \int_0^1 r \left( 2r^2 \right) dr \, d\theta\]
        \[= \int_0^{2\pi} \left( \int_0^1 2r^3 \, dr \right) d\theta\]
        \[= \int_0^{2\pi} \left[ \frac{r^4}{2} \right]_0^1 d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{1}{2} d\theta\]
        \[= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\pi = \pi\]

        \[r^2 - 2r \sin \theta = 0 \quad \implies \quad r^2 = 2r \sin \theta \quad \implies \quad r = 2 \sin \theta\]

Then use the Jacobian to find the area of the image of the rectangle \( R = [0, 4] \times[0, 3] \) under \( T \).

        Solution to exercise 44
        \[ T(u,v)=(3u+v,-u+4v),\qquad\det DT=\begin{vmatrix}3&1\\-1&4\end{vmatrix}=13. \]\[ \operatorname{Area}(T([0,4]\times[0,3]))=13\cdot4\cdot3=156. \]
      2. 45Chapter 5

        Use the map

        \[G(u, v) = \left( \frac{u + v}{2}, \frac{u - v}{2} \right)\]
        to compute
        \[\iint\limits_R ((x - y) \sin(x + y))^2 \, dx \, dy,\]
        where \( R \) is the square with vertices \( (\pi, 0) \), \( (2\pi, \pi) \), \( (\pi, 2\pi) \), and \( (0, \pi) \).
        Solution to exercise 45
        \[ u=x+y,\quad v=x-y,\qquad|\det DG|=\left|\begin{vmatrix}1/2&1/2\\1/2&-1/2\end{vmatrix}\right|=\frac12. \]

        The vertices map to \((\pi,\pi),(3\pi,\pi),(3\pi,-\pi),(\pi,-\pi)\). Thus

        \[ I=\frac12\int_\pi^{3\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi v^2\sin^2u\,dv\,du=\frac12\cdot\pi\cdot\frac{2\pi^3}{3}=\frac{\pi^4}{3}. \]
      3. 46Chapter 5

        Let \( D \) be the shaded region in the figure below, and let \( F \) be the map

        \[u = y + x^2, \quad v = y - x^3\]

        1. Show that \( F \) maps \( D \) to a rectangle \( R \) in the \( uv \)-plane.

        2. Apply the formula \(\text{Area}(F(D)) \approx \abs{\text{Jac}(F)(P)} \text{Area}(D)\) with \( P = (1, 7) \) to estimate Area(\( D \)).

        Solution to exercise 46

        The four boundaries \(y=x^3+5,y=x^3+6,y=8-x^2,y=9-x^2\) map to \(v=5,6\) and \(u=8,9\), giving \(R=[8,9]\times[5,6]\).

        \[ \det DF=\begin{vmatrix}2x&1\\-3x^2&1\end{vmatrix}=2x+3x^2,\qquad\det DF(1,7)=5. \]\[ \operatorname{Area}(D)\approx\frac{\operatorname{Area}(R)}{|\det DF(1,7)|}=\frac15. \]

        Solution to exercise 47
