Vector fields, divergence & curl

1–8
  1. 01Chapter 6
    Sketch the vector field \(\mathbf{F}(x,y)= \langle y,1 \rangle\).
    Solution to exercise 1
    Notice that the vector field is constant along horizontal lines.
  2. 02Chapter 6
    Sketch the vector field \(\nabla f\), where \(f(x,y)=x^2-y\).
    Solution to exercise 2
    Solution. The gradient of \(f(x,y)=x^2-y\) is \[ \mathbf{F}(x,y) = \left\langle \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \right\rangle = \langle 2x, -1 \rangle. \]
  3. 03Chapter 6
    Compute the divergence and the curl of the vector field \(\mathbf{F} = \langle x^3y,\, xz^2,\, y^2z \rangle\).
    Solution to exercise 3
    Solution. The divergence is \[ \operatorname{div}(\mathbf{F}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^3y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(xz^2) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y^2z) = 3x^2y + y^2. \] The curl is \[ \operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{F}) = \nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ x^3y & xz^2 & y^2z \end{vmatrix} \] \[ = \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2z) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(xz^2)\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y^2z) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x^3y)\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xz^2) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^3y)\right)\mathbf{k} \] \[ = (2yz - 2xz)\mathbf{i} + (z^2 - x^3)\mathbf{k}. \]
  4. 04Chapter 6
    Compute the divergence and the curl of the vector field \(\mathbf{F} = y\mathbf{i} - z\mathbf{k}\).
    Solution to exercise 4
    Solution. The divergence is \[ \operatorname{div}(\mathbf{F}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(0) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(-z) = 0 + 0 - 1 = -1. \] The curl is \[ \operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{F}) = \nabla \times \mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ y & 0 & -z \end{vmatrix} \] \[ = \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-z) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(0)\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(-z) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(y)\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(0) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y)\right)\mathbf{k} \] \[ = 0\mathbf{i} + 0\mathbf{j} - 1\mathbf{k} = -\mathbf{k}. \]
  5. 05Chapter 6
    Compute the divergence and the curl of the vector field \(\mathbf{F} = \langle e^{x+y}, e^{y+z}, xyz \rangle\).
    Solution to exercise 5
    Solution. The divergence is \[ \operatorname{div}(\mathbf{F}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(e^{x+y}) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(e^{y+z}) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(xyz) = e^{x+y} + e^{y+z} + xy. \] The curl is \[ \operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{F}) = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ e^{x+y} & e^{y+z} & xyz \end{vmatrix} \] \[ = \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(xyz) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(e^{y+z})\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(xyz) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(e^{x+y})\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(e^{y+z}) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(e^{x+y})\right)\mathbf{k} \] \[ = (xz - e^{y+z})\mathbf{i} - (yz)\mathbf{j} - e^{x+y}\mathbf{k} = \langle xz - e^{y+z},\, -yz,\, -e^{x+y} \rangle. \]
  6. 06Chapter 6
    Show that if \(P(x)\), \(Q(y)\), and \(R(z)\) are differentiable functions of one variable, then \[ \operatorname{curl}\!\left(\langle P(x), Q(y), R(z)\rangle\right) = \mathbf{0}. \] Use this to calculate the curl of \[ \mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle x^2+y^2,\; \ln y + z^2,\; z^3\sin(z^2)e^{z^3} \rangle. \]
    Solution to exercise 6
    Solution. We compute the curl directly: \[ \operatorname{curl}\langle P(x), Q(y), R(z)\rangle = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ P(x) & Q(y) & R(z) \end{vmatrix} \] \[ = \left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z}\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial R}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)\mathbf{k}. \] Since \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\) depend only on their respective variables, all cross-partial derivatives are \(0\), so \(\operatorname{curl}\langle P,Q,R\rangle = \mathbf{0}\).

    Now we use linearity of the curl: \[ \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{F} = \operatorname{curl}\langle x^2+y^2, \ln y+z^2, z^3\sin(z^2)e^{z^3}\rangle \] \[ = \operatorname{curl}\langle x^2, \ln y, z^3\sin(z^2)e^{z^3}\rangle + \operatorname{curl}\langle y^2, z^2, 0\rangle = \mathbf{0} + \operatorname{curl}\langle y^2, z^2, 0\rangle. \] Computing the remaining curl: \[ \operatorname{curl}\langle y^2, z^2, 0\rangle = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ y^2 & z^2 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \langle -2z, 0, -2y\rangle. \]
  7. 07Chapter 6
    Give an example of a nonzero vector field \(\mathbf{F}\) such that \(\operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{F}) = \mathbf{0}\) and \(\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{F}) = 0\).
    Solution to exercise 7
    Solution. Let \(\mathbf{F} = \langle x, -y, 0\rangle\). Then \[ \operatorname{curl}(\mathbf{F}) = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ x & -y & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(0) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(-y)\right)\mathbf{i} - \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(0) - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x)\right)\mathbf{j} + \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(-y) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x)\right)\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0}. \] \[ \operatorname{div}(\mathbf{F}) = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(0) = 1 - 1 + 0 = 0. \]
  8. 08Chapter 6
    Verify the identity \(\operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{F}) = 0\) for \(\mathbf{F} = \langle xz, ye^x, yz\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{G} = \langle z^2, xy^3, x^2y\rangle\).
    Solution to exercise 8
    Solution. For \(\mathbf{F}\): \[ \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ xz & ye^x & yz \end{vmatrix} = (z-0)\mathbf{i} - (0-x)\mathbf{j} + (ye^x-0)\mathbf{k} = z\mathbf{i} + x\mathbf{j} + ye^x\mathbf{k}. \] \[ \operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{F}) = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial x}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial(ye^x)}{\partial z} = 0. \] For \(\mathbf{G}\): \[ \operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{G} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ z^2 & xy^3 & x^2y \end{vmatrix} = (x^2-0)\mathbf{i} + (2z-2xy)\mathbf{j}+(y^3-0)\mathbf{k} = x^2\mathbf{i} + (2z-2xy)\mathbf{j} + y^3\mathbf{k}. \] \[ \operatorname{div}(\operatorname{curl}\,\mathbf{G}) = \frac{\partial(x^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial(2z-2xy)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial(y^3)}{\partial z} = 2x + (-2x) + 0 = 0. \]

Conservative fields & potentials

9–12

    Determine whether each field is conservative, and if so, find a potential function.

    1. 09Chapter 6
      \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle x^2y,\, y^2x \rangle\)
      Solution to exercise 9
      Solution. Check cross-partials: \[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^2y) = x^2, \qquad \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(y^2x) = y^2. \] Since \(x^2 \neq y^2\) in general, \(\mathbf{F}\) is not conservative.
    2. 10Chapter 6
      \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \langle \sin x,\, e^y,\, z\rangle\)
      Solution to exercise 10
      Solution. Since \(P = \sin x\), \(Q = e^y\), \(R = z\), all cross-partials are zero and equal, so \(\mathbf{F}\) is conservative. Integrating each component: \[ V = -\cos x + e^y + \tfrac{1}{2}z^2. \]
    3. 11Chapter 6
      \(\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle xyz,\, \tfrac{1}{2}x^2z,\, 2z^2y\right\rangle\)
      Solution to exercise 11
      Solution. Check one pair of cross-partials: \[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(xyz) = xy, \qquad \frac{\partial R}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2z^2y) = 0. \] Since \(xy \neq 0\) in general, \(\mathbf{F}\) is not conservative.
    4. 12Chapter 6
      \(\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle \frac{y}{1+x^2},\, \tan^{-1}x,\, 2z\right\rangle\)
      Solution to exercise 12
      Solution. All three cross-partial conditions hold: \[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{1+x^2} = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}, \qquad \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z} = 0 = \frac{\partial R}{\partial y}, \qquad \frac{\partial R}{\partial x} = 0 = \frac{\partial P}{\partial z}. \] So \(\mathbf{F}\) is conservative. Integrating: \[ V(x,y,z) = y\tan^{-1}(x) + z^2. \]

    Scalar line integrals

    13–17

      In Exercises 13–16, compute the scalar line integral \(\int_C f\,ds\).

      1. 13Chapter 6
        \(f(x,y) = xy\), path \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle t, 2t-1\rangle\), \(0\le t\le 1\).
        Solution to exercise 13
        Step 1. \(\mathbf{r}'(t) = \langle 1,2\rangle\), so \(\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{5}\). Step 2. \(f(\mathbf{r}(t)) = t(2t-1) = 2t^2-t\). \[ \int_C f\,ds = \int_0^1(2t^2-t)\sqrt{5}\,dt = \sqrt{5}\left[\frac{2}{3}t^3-\frac{1}{2}t^2\right]_0^1 = \sqrt{5}\cdot\frac{1}{6} = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{6}. \]
      2. 14Chapter 6
        \(f(x,y) = x-y\), the unit semicircle \(x^2+y^2=1\), \(y\ge 0\).
        Solution to exercise 14
        Parametrize by \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle\cos t,\sin t\rangle\), \(0\le t\le\pi\). Then \(\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = 1\) and \(f(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \cos t - \sin t\). \[ \int_C f\,ds = \int_0^\pi(\cos t - \sin t)\,dt = \left[\sin t + \cos t\right]_0^\pi = (0-1)-(0+1) = -2. \]
      3. 15Chapter 6
        \(\displaystyle f(x,y,z) = e^x - \frac{y}{2\sqrt{2}\,z}\), path \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \left\langle\ln t,\,\sqrt{2}\,t,\,\tfrac{1}{2}t^2\right\rangle\), \(1\le t\le 2\).
        Solution to exercise 15
        Step 1. \[ \mathbf{r}'(t) = \left\langle\frac{1}{t},\sqrt{2},t\right\rangle, \quad \|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{\frac{1}{t^2}+2+t^2} = \frac{1}{t}+t. \] Step 2. \[ f(\mathbf{r}(t)) = e^{\ln t} - \frac{\sqrt{2}\,t}{2\sqrt{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}t^2} = t - \frac{1}{t}. \] \[ \int_C f\,ds = \int_1^2\left(t-\frac{1}{t}\right)\left(t+\frac{1}{t}\right)dt = \int_1^2\left(t^2-\frac{1}{t^2}\right)dt = \left[\frac{t^3}{3}+\frac{1}{t}\right]_1^2 = \left(\frac{8}{3}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{1}{3}+1\right) = \frac{11}{6}. \]
      4. 16Chapter 6
        \(f(x,y,z) = x+2y+z\), the helix \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle\cos t,\sin t,t\rangle\), \(-1\le t\le 3\).
        Solution to exercise 16
        \(\|\mathbf{r}'(t)\| = \sqrt{\sin^2t+\cos^2t+1} = \sqrt{2}\), and \(f(\mathbf{r}(t)) = \cos t+2\sin t+t\). \[ \int_C f\,ds = \sqrt{2}\int_{-1}^3(\cos t+2\sin t+t)\,dt = \sqrt{2}\left[\sin t - 2\cos t+\frac{t^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^3 \approx 11.375. \]
      5. 17Chapter 6
        Find the total mass of an L-shaped rod consisting of the segments \(\langle 2t, 2\rangle\) and \(\langle 2, 2-2t\rangle\) for \(0\le t\le 1\) (length in centimeters) with mass density \(\rho(x,y) = x^2y\ \text{g/cm}\).
        Solution to exercise 17
        Solution. \[M = \int_{AB} x^2y\,ds + \int_{BC} x^2y\,ds.\] Segment \(AB\): \(\mathbf{c}_1(t)=(2t,2)\), \(\|\mathbf{c}_1'\|=2\), \(f=8t^2\). Segment \(BC\): \(\mathbf{c}_2(t)=(2,2-2t)\), \(\|\mathbf{c}_2'\|=2\), \(f=8-8t\). \[ M = \int_0^1 8t^2\cdot 2\,dt + \int_0^1(8-8t)\cdot 2\,dt = \frac{16t^3}{3}\bigg|_0^1 + \left(16t-8t^2\right)\bigg|_0^1 = \frac{16}{3}+8 = \frac{40}{3} \approx 13.\overline{3}\ \text{g}. \]

      Vector line integrals & work

      18–26
      1. 18Chapter 6
        Calculate \(\mathbf{F} = \nabla f\) where \(f(x,y,z)=xyze^z\), and compute \(\displaystyle\int_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) for:
        1. \(C\) is any curve from \((1,1,0)\) to \((3,e,-1)\).
        2. \(C\) is the boundary of the square \(0\le x\le 1\), \(0\le y\le 1\), oriented counterclockwise.
        Solution to exercise 18
        \[ \nabla(xyze^z)=\langle yze^z,xze^z,xy(1+z)e^z\rangle. \]\[ \int_C\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf r=f(3,e,-1)-f(1,1,0)=-3-0=-3. \]

        For the closed square, the integral is 0.

      2. 19Chapter 6
        Calculate \(\displaystyle\int_{C_1} y\,dx + x^2y\,dy\), where \(C_1\) is the oriented curve below (path \(A\to O\to B\to\) arc back to \(A\), where \(O=(0,0)\), \(A=(0,3)\), \(B=(3,0)\)).
        Solution to exercise 19
        Solution. \(\mathbf{F} = \langle y, x^2y\rangle\). We split \(C_1 = \overline{AO} + \overline{OB} + \text{arc }BA\).
        • Segment \(\overline{AO}\): \(\mathbf{r}(t)=(0,-t)\), \(-3\le t\le 0\). \(\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}'=0\), so the integral is \(0\).
        • Segment \(\overline{OB}\): \(\mathbf{r}(t)=(t,0)\), \(0\le t\le 3\). \(\mathbf{F}=\langle 0,0\rangle\), so the integral is \(0\).
        • Arc \(BA\): \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\langle 3\cos t, 3\sin t\rangle\), \(0\le t\le\pi/2\). Then \[ \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}' = -9\sin^2t + 81\cos^3t\sin t. \] \[ \int_{\text{arc}} = \int_0^{\pi/2}\left(-9\sin^2t+81\cos^3t\sin t\right)dt = -9\frac\pi4+81\frac14 = \frac{9}{4}(9-\pi). \]
        Therefore \(\displaystyle\int_{C_1}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \dfrac{9}{4}(9-\pi)\).
      3. 20Chapter 6
        Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \left(9y-y^3,\; e^{\sqrt{y}}(x^2-3x)\right)\), and let \(C_2\) be the boundary of the square \([0,3]\times[0,3]\), oriented counterclockwise.
        1. Show that \(\mathbf{F}\) is not conservative.
        2. Show that \(\displaystyle\int_{C_2}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0\) without computing the integral explicitly.
        Solution to exercise 20
        (a) With \(P=9y-y^3\) and \(Q=e^{\sqrt{y}}(x^2-3x)\): \[ \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 9-3y^2 \neq e^{\sqrt{y}}(2x-3) = \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}, \] so \(\mathbf{F}\) is not conservative.
        (b) On each side of the square \(\mathbf{F}\) is orthogonal to the path:
        • \(\overrightarrow{OA}\) (\(y=0\)): \(\mathbf{F}=\langle 0, x^2-3x\rangle\), which is perpendicular to the horizontal direction.
        • \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) (\(x=3\)): \(\mathbf{F}=\langle 9y-y^3, 0\rangle\), perpendicular to the vertical direction.
        • \(\overrightarrow{BC}\) (\(y=3\)): \(\mathbf{F}=\langle 0, e^{\sqrt{3}}(x^2-3x)\rangle\), perpendicular to the horizontal direction.
        • \(\overrightarrow{CO}\) (\(x=0\)): \(\mathbf{F}=\langle 9y-y^3, 0\rangle\), perpendicular to the vertical direction.
        Hence each segment contributes \(0\) and the total integral is \(0\).

      Compute the vector line integral \(\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) in Exercises 21–24.

      1. 21Chapter 6
        \(\displaystyle\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \left\langle\frac{2y}{x^2+4y^2},\;\frac{-x}{x^2+4y^2}\right\rangle\), path \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\left\langle\cos t,\,\tfrac{1}{2}\sin t\right\rangle\), \(0\le t\le 2\pi\).

        \[ \mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))=\langle\sin t,-\cos t\rangle,\qquad\mathbf r'(t)=\langle-\sin t,\tfrac12\cos t\rangle. \]\[ \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r'=-\sin^2t-\frac12\cos^2t,\qquad\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=-\pi-\frac\pi2=-\frac{3\pi}{2}. \]
      2. 22Chapter 6
        \(\mathbf{F}(x,y) = \langle 2xy,\, x^2+y^2\rangle\), the part of the unit circle in the first quadrant oriented counterclockwise.
        Solution to exercise 22
        Parametrize: \(\mathbf{r}(\theta)=\langle\cos\theta,\sin\theta\rangle\), \(0\le\theta\le\pi/2\). \[ \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}' = -2\cos\theta\sin^2\theta+\cos\theta. \] \[ \int_C = \int_0^{\pi/2}(-2\cos\theta\sin^2\theta+\cos\theta)\,d\theta = -2\cdot\frac{\sin^3\theta}{3}\bigg|_0^{\pi/2} + 1 = -\frac{2}{3}+1 = \frac{1}{3}. \]
      3. 23Chapter 6
        \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=\langle x^2y, y^2z, z^2x\rangle\), path \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\langle e^{-t}, e^{-2t}, e^{-3t}\rangle\), \(0\le t<\infty\).
        Solution to exercise 23
        \(\mathbf{r}'(t)=\langle -e^{-t},-2e^{-2t},-3e^{-3t}\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))=\langle e^{-4t}, e^{-7t}, e^{-7t}\rangle\). \[ \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}' = -e^{-5t}-2e^{-9t}-3e^{-10t}. \] \[ \int_C = \int_0^\infty(-e^{-5t}-2e^{-9t}-3e^{-10t})\,dt = -\frac{1}{5}-\frac{2}{9}-\frac{3}{10} = -\frac{13}{18}. \]
      4. 24Chapter 6
        \(\mathbf{F}=\nabla f\) where \(f(x,y,z)=4x^2\ln(1+y^4+z^2)\), path \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\left\langle t^3,\ln(1+t^2),e^t\right\rangle\), \(0\le t\le 1\).
        Solution to exercise 24
        By the Fundamental Theorem: \[ \int_C\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf{r} = f(\mathbf{r}(1))-f(\mathbf{r}(0)). \] \(\mathbf{r}(1)=\langle 1,\ln 2, e\rangle\), \(\mathbf{r}(0)=\langle 0,0,1\rangle\). Then \(f(\mathbf{r}(0))=0\) and \[ f(\mathbf{r}(1)) = 4\ln\!\left(1+e^2+(\ln2)^4\right). \] \[ \int_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 4\ln\!\left(1+e^2+(\ln2)^4\right). \]
      5. 25Chapter 6
        Consider the line integrals \(\displaystyle\int_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) for the vector fields and paths shown in the figure. Which two appear to have value zero? Which appears to be negative?
        Solution to exercise 25

      6. 26Chapter 6
        Calculate the work required to move an object from \(P=(1,1,1)\) to \(Q=(3,-4,-2)\) against the force field \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=-12r^{-4}\langle x,y,z\rangle\) where \(r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\). Hint: Find a potential function.
        Solution to exercise 26
        Observe that \(\mathbf{F}=\nabla f\) with \(f(x,y,z)=\dfrac{6}{x^2+y^2+z^2}\). Then \[ W = -\int_{\overline{PQ}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = -(f(Q)-f(P)) = -\left(\frac{6}{29}-\frac{6}{3}\right) = \frac{52}{29}\approx 1.79\ \text{N·m}. \]

      Surface geometry & integrals

      27–31
      1. 27Chapter 6
        Calculate \(\displaystyle\iint_S e^z\,dS\), where \(S\) is the portion of the plane \(x+2y+2z=3\) with \(x,y,z\ge 0\).
        Solution to exercise 27
        Write \(z=g(x,y)=\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{x}{2}-y\). Then \(\sqrt{1+g_x^2+g_y^2}=\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{4}+1}=\tfrac{3}{2}\). \[ \iint_S e^z\,dS = \frac{3}{2}e^{3/2}\int_0^3\int_0^{(3-x)/2} e^{-x/2}e^{-y}\,dy\,dx = -\frac{15}{2}+3e^{3/2}. \]
      2. 28Chapter 6
        Let \(S\) be parametrized by \(\mathbf{r}(u,v)=\left\langle 2u\sin\tfrac{v}{2},\,-2u\cos\tfrac{v}{2},\,-3v\right\rangle\), \(0\le u\le 1\), \(0\le v\le 2\pi\).
        1. Find \(\mathbf{r}_u\), \(\mathbf{r}_v\), and the normal \(\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{r}_u\times\mathbf{r}_v\) at \(P=\mathbf{r}(1,\pi/3)\).
        2. Find the equation of the tangent plane at \(P\).
        3. Compute the surface area of \(S\).
        Solution to exercise 28
        \[ \mathbf r_u=\langle2\sin(v/2),-2\cos(v/2),0\rangle,\quad\mathbf r_v=\langle u\cos(v/2),u\sin(v/2),-3\rangle. \]\[ \mathbf r_u\times\mathbf r_v=\langle6\cos(v/2),6\sin(v/2),2u\rangle. \]\[ P=(1,-\sqrt3,-\pi),\qquad\mathbf n(P)=\langle3\sqrt3,3,2\rangle. \]\[ 3\sqrt3(x-1)+3(y+\sqrt3)+2(z+\pi)=0. \]\[ A=\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^12\sqrt{9+u^2}\,du\,dv=2\pi\sqrt{10}+18\pi\ln\frac{1+\sqrt{10}}3. \]
      3. 29Chapter 6
        Evaluate \(\displaystyle\iint_S x^2y\,dS\), where \(S\) is \(z=\sqrt{3}\,x+y^2\), \(-1\le x\le 1\), \(0\le y\le 1\).
        Solution to exercise 29
        \[ dS=2\sqrt{1+y^2}\,dx\,dy,\qquad I=\left(\int_{-1}^1x^2dx\right)\left(\int_0^12y\sqrt{1+y^2}dy\right). \]\[ I=\frac23\cdot\frac23(2\sqrt2-1)=\frac49(2\sqrt2-1). \]
      4. 30Chapter 6
        Calculate \(\displaystyle\iint_S(x^2+y^2)e^{-z}\,dS\), where \(S\) is the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=9\), \(0\le z\le 10\). Use \(\mathbf{r}(\theta,z)=\langle 3\cos\theta,3\sin\theta,z\rangle\).
        Solution to exercise 30
        \(\|\mathbf{r}_\theta\times\mathbf{r}_z\| = 3\). On the surface \(x^2+y^2=9\), so \(f(\mathbf{r}(\theta,z))=9e^{-z}\). \[ \iint_S = \int_0^{10}\int_0^{2\pi}9e^{-z}\cdot 3\,d\theta\,dz = 54\pi(1-e^{-10})\approx 54\pi. \]
      5. 31Chapter 6
        Let \(S\) be the upper hemisphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\), \(z\ge 0\). Determine whether \(\displaystyle\iint_S f\,dS\) is positive, zero, or negative (without computing) for each of the following:
        1. \(f(x,y,z)=y^3\)
        2. \(f(x,y,z)=z^3\)
        3. \(f(x,y,z)=xy^2\)
        4. \(f(x,y,z)=z^2-2\)
        Solution to exercise 31
        1. Zero — \(y^3\) is odd in \(y\); the hemisphere is symmetric about the \(xz\)-plane.
        2. Positive — \(z^3\ge 0\) on the upper hemisphere.
        3. Zero — \(f(-x,y,z)=-f(x,y,z)\); symmetric about the \(yz\)-plane.
        4. Negative — on \(S\), \(z^2=1-x^2-y^2\le 1\), so \(z^2-2<0\).

          Solution to exercise 34

        4. 35Chapter 6

          Recall: \(\sinh x = \tfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}\), \(\cosh x=\tfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}\), \(\cosh^2-\sinh^2=1\).
          Solution to exercise 35
          \(\mathbf{r}_u=\langle v\sinh u,v\cosh u,0\rangle\), \(\mathbf{r}_v=\langle\cosh u,\sinh u,1\rangle\). \[ \mathbf{r}_u\times\mathbf{r}_v = \langle v\cosh u,-v\sinh u,-v\rangle. \] Since the upward normal needs positive \(z\)-component: \(\mathbf{n}=\langle -v\cosh u,v\sinh u,v\rangle\). \[ \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v))=\langle v,0,v^2\rangle. \] \[ \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n} = -v^2\cosh u+v^3. \] \[ \iint_S = \int_0^1\int_0^1(-v^2\cosh u+v^3)\,du\,dv = -\frac{\sinh 1}{3}+\frac{1}{4}\approx -0.1417. \]
        5. 36Chapter 6
          \(\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=\langle 0,0,xze^{xy}\rangle\), \(z=xy\), \(0\le x,y\le 1\), upward normal.
          Solution to exercise 36
          Parametrize by \(\mathbf{r}(x,y)=\langle x,y,xy\rangle\).           Solution to exercise 37
          After computing \(\mathbf{r}_\theta\times\mathbf{r}_\phi\) and taking the upward-pointing normal: \[ \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\sin\phi\cos^2\phi. \] \[ \iint_S = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\sin\phi\cos^2\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta = \frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{6}}\cdot\frac{1}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{2}\,\pi}{3\sqrt{3}}. \]
        7. 38Chapter 6
          Calculate the flux of \(\mathbf{E}(x,y,z)=\langle 0,0,x\rangle\) through the part of the ellipsoid \(4x^2+9y^2+z^2=36\) where \(z\ge 3\), \(x>0\), \(y>0\). Use the parametrization \(\mathbf{q}(r,\theta)=\langle 3r\cos\theta,2r\sin\theta,6\sqrt{1-r^2}\rangle\).
          Solution to exercise 38
          The condition \(z\ge 3\) gives \(0\le r\le\sqrt{3}/2\).         Green’s Theorem

        39–44
        1. 39Chapter 6
          Let \(\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\langle x+y^2,\,x^2-y\rangle\) and \(C\) be the unit circle oriented counterclockwise. Evaluate \(\oint_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) directly and using Green's Theorem.
          Solution to exercise 39
          Direct calculation: Parametrize by \(\mathbf{r}(t)=(\cos t,\sin t)\). The integrand \(\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{r}' = \cos^3t-\sin^3t-2\sin t\cos t\), and integrating over \([0,2\pi]\) gives \(0\). Green's Theorem: \[ \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 2x-2y. \] \[ \iint_D(2x-2y)\,dA = \int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^1 2r^2(\cos\theta-\sin\theta)\,dr\,d\theta = \frac{2}{3}\cdot 0 = 0. \]
        2. 40Chapter 6
          Let \(\partial R\) be the boundary of the rectangle, and \(\partial R_1\), \(\partial R_2\) the boundaries of two triangles (all counterclockwise).
          1. Find \(\displaystyle\oint_{\partial R_1}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) if \(\displaystyle\oint_{\partial R}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=4\) and \(\displaystyle\oint_{\partial R_2}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=-2\).
          2. What is \(\displaystyle\oint_{\partial R}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}\) if \(\partial R\) is oriented clockwise?
          Solution to exercise 40
          (a) The diagonal \(\overline{DB}\) is traversed in opposite directions by \(\partial R_1\) and \(\partial R_2\), so their contributions cancel:
          \[ \oint_{\partial R} = \oint_{\partial R_1} + \oint_{\partial R_2} \implies 4 = \oint_{\partial R_1} - 2 \implies \oint_{\partial R_1} = 6. \] (b) Reversing orientation negates the integral: \(\oint_{\partial R,\text{cw}} = -4\).

        1. 50Chapter 6
          In (a)–(d), state whether the equation is an identity. If not, give a counterexample.

          \(\mathbf F=\langle\sin(yz),\sqrt{x^2+z^4},x\cos(x-y)\rangle\). Find the outward flux across any smooth closed surface bounding a region in \(\mathbb R^3\).

          Solution to exercise 61
          \[ \operatorname{div}\mathbf F=0+0+0=0,\qquad\iint_S\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf S=0. \]

          The second component is not continuously differentiable everywhere. For a direct justification, replace it by \(\sqrt{x^2+z^4+\varepsilon^2}\).
