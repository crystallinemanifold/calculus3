Directions

Read each statement carefully. Circle every true statement and cross out every false one. A problem may have one true statement, several, or all five.

Mark every statement. A statement left unmarked counts as wrong.

Use ○ to circle and ✕ to cross out; click again to undo. The exam has six problems in 35 minutes, so aim for about six minutes per problem. Do a whole problem before checking it.

How to approach each statement

Treat every statement under a problem as its own small question, and do not decide it by how it looks. Each statement is settled by exactly one of four things:

  • Definition such as the length, unit vector, dot product, cross product, or a quadric surface type;
  • Theorem or property, such as the triangle inequality, anticommutativity of the cross product, or the product rules;
  • Formula such as \(\cos\theta=\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w}{\|\mathbf v\|\|\mathbf w\|}\), \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v\), arc length, or a coordinate conversion;
  • Calculation to carry out: compute the components, solve the equations, differentiate or integrate.

Before you circle or cross out, name which one applies, then use it. If it is a calculation, do the calculation.

Example. Problem 1(b) claims \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|=\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|\). That is a claim about a property of lengths. The theorem that applies is the triangle inequality: \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|\le\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|\), with equality only when one vector is a nonnegative multiple of the other. These two vectors point in different directions, so the statement is false. The calculation confirms it: \(\|\langle-1,3\rangle\|=\sqrt{10}\), not \(5+\sqrt5\).

Every solution below is labeled with the definition, theorem, formula, or calculation that decides it.

Vectors & vector operations

Problems 1–5
  1. 01

    Let \(\mathbf v=\langle-3,4\rangle\) and \(\mathbf w=\langle2,-1\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      The unit vector in the direction of \(\mathbf v\) is \(\left\langle-\frac35,\frac45\right\rangle\).
    2. (b)
      \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|=\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|\).
    3. (c)
      \(2\mathbf v-3\mathbf w=\langle-12,11\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      \(\mathbf i=\frac15\mathbf v+\frac45\mathbf w\).
    5. (e)
      \(\|\mathbf v\|=5\).
    Solution to problem 1

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition unit vector

      \(\frac{\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|}=\frac15\langle-3,4\rangle\), which has length \(1\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Theorem triangle inequality

      The triangle inequality says \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|\le\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|\), with equality only when one vector is a nonnegative multiple of the other. Here \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|=\|\langle-1,3\rangle\|=\sqrt{10}\approx3.16\), while \(\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|=5+\sqrt5\approx7.24\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(\langle-6,8\rangle-\langle6,-3\rangle=\langle-12,11\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      Solve \(-3r+2s=1\), \(4r-s=0\): \(s=4r\), so \(5r=1\), \(r=\frac15\), \(s=\frac45\). Check: \(\frac15\langle-3,4\rangle+\frac45\langle2,-1\rangle=\langle1,0\rangle\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\sqrt{(-3)^2+4^2}=\sqrt{25}=5\).
  2. 02

    Let \(P=(2,-1,3)\) and \(Q=(5,1,-3)\).

    1. (a)
      \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=\langle3,2,-6\rangle\).
    2. (b)
      \(\|\overrightarrow{PQ}\|=7\).
    3. (c)
      \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=\overrightarrow{QP}\).
    4. (d)
      \(\left\langle\frac37,\frac27,-\frac67\right\rangle\) is a unit vector parallel to \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\).
    5. (e)
      The midpoint of the segment \(PQ\) is \(\left(\frac32,1,-3\right)\).
    Solution to problem 2

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition vector between points

      \(\overrightarrow{PQ}=Q-P=\langle5-2,\,1-(-1),\,-3-3\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(\sqrt{9+4+36}=\sqrt{49}=7\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Definition vector between points

      \(\overrightarrow{QP}=P-Q=-\overrightarrow{PQ}\): same length, opposite direction.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Definition unit vector

      Divide \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) by its length \(7\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      The midpoint is \(\frac{P+Q}{2}=\left(\frac72,0,0\right)\). The point \(\left(\frac32,1,-3\right)\) is \(\frac12\overrightarrow{PQ}\), a vector, not the midpoint.
  3. 03

    The following statements concern scalar multiples and parallel vectors.

    1. (a)
      \(\langle1,2,3\rangle\) and \(\langle2,4,5\rangle\) are parallel.
    2. (b)
      \(\langle1,2,-1\rangle\) and \(\langle-3,-6,-3\rangle\) are parallel.
    3. (c)
      \(\langle-2,\beta\rangle\) is parallel to \(\langle4,-6\rangle\) when \(\beta=3\).
    4. (d)
      If \(\mathbf v+5\mathbf u=3\mathbf w-\mathbf u\), then \(\mathbf u=\frac{3\mathbf w-\mathbf v}{4}\).
    5. (e)
      The vector of length \(4\) making an angle \(\frac{5\pi}{6}\) with the positive \(x\)-axis is \(\langle-2\sqrt3,\,2\rangle\).
    Solution to problem 3

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Definition parallel vectors

      The ratios \(\frac21,\frac42,\frac53\) are not all equal.
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition parallel vectors

      A multiple of \(\langle1,2,-1\rangle\) with first entry \(-3\) is \(\langle-3,-6,3\rangle\); the last entries disagree.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition parallel vectors

      Parallel means one is a scalar multiple of the other: \(\langle-2,3\rangle=-\frac12\langle4,-6\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      Collect the \(\mathbf u\) terms: \(6\mathbf u=3\mathbf w-\mathbf v\), so \(\mathbf u=\frac{3\mathbf w-\mathbf v}{6}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Formula vector from length and angle

      \(\langle4\cos\frac{5\pi}{6},\,4\sin\frac{5\pi}{6}\rangle=\langle-2\sqrt3,\,2\rangle\).
  4. 04

    Let \(\mathbf v=\langle1,2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf w=\langle2,-1\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|=2\sqrt5\).
    2. (b)
      Both \(\alpha=2\) and \(\alpha=-2\) give \(\|\mathbf v+\alpha\mathbf w\|=5\).
    3. (c)
      \(\|-3\mathbf v\|=-3\|\mathbf v\|\).
    4. (d)
      \(3(\mathbf i-2\mathbf j)-6(\mathbf i+6\mathbf j)=-3\mathbf i+30\mathbf j\).
    5. (e)
      \(\langle4,3\rangle=2\mathbf v+\mathbf w\).
    Solution to problem 4

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(\mathbf v+\mathbf w=\langle3,1\rangle\), with length \(\sqrt{10}\). (\(2\sqrt5=\|\mathbf v\|+\|\mathbf w\|\), which would require \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) to point the same way.)
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(\|\mathbf v+\alpha\mathbf w\|^2=(1+2\alpha)^2+(2-\alpha)^2=5+5\alpha^2\), which equals \(25\) when \(\alpha^2=4\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem length of a scalar multiple

      \(\|c\mathbf v\|=|c|\,\|\mathbf v\|\), so \(\|-3\mathbf v\|=3\sqrt5\). A length is never negative.
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(3\mathbf i-6\mathbf j-6\mathbf i-36\mathbf j=-3\mathbf i-42\mathbf j\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\langle2,4\rangle+\langle2,-1\rangle=\langle4,3\rangle\).
  5. 05

    The following statements concern basic properties of vectors.

    1. (a)
      If \(\|\mathbf v\|=0\), then \(\mathbf v=\mathbf 0\).
    2. (b)
      If \(A=(2,-1)\), \(B=(1,4)\), and \(P=(2,3)\), the point \(Q\) with \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) equivalent to \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) is \((1,8)\).
    3. (c)
      For every scalar \(c\), \(\|c\mathbf v\|=|c|\,\|\mathbf v\|\).
    4. (d)
      \(\overrightarrow{PQ}\) is equivalent to \(\overrightarrow{AB}\) exactly when \(Q-P=B-A\).
    5. (e)
      Two vectors with the same length are equal.
    Solution to problem 5

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition length

      \(\|\mathbf v\|^2\) is a sum of squares of the components, which is \(0\) only when every component is \(0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(Q=P+(B-A)=(2,3)+\langle-1,5\rangle=(1,8)\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem length of a scalar multiple

      Each component is multiplied by \(c\), so the length is multiplied by \(\sqrt{c^2}=|c|\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Definition equivalent vectors

      Equivalent vectors have the same components, which means the same length and direction.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Definition equal vectors

      Equal vectors need the same direction as well; \(\langle1,0\rangle\) and \(\langle0,1\rangle\) both have length \(1\).

Problems 6–9
  1. 06

    Let \(L\) be the line through \((1,4,5)\) and \((-2,3,-1)\), parametrized by \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle1,4,5\rangle+t\langle-3,-1,-6\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(L\) passes through \((4,5,11)\).
    2. (b)
      \(L\) passes through \((-2,3,-1)\).
    3. (c)
      \(L\) passes through \((7,6,17)\).
    4. (d)
      \(\langle-2,3,-1\rangle+t\langle3,1,6\rangle\) is another parametrization of \(L\).
    5. (e)
      \(\langle1,0,0\rangle+t\langle6,2,12\rangle\) is a line parallel to \(L\).
    Solution to problem 6

    Key: all five statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(t=-1\) gives \(\langle4,5,11\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(t=1\) gives \(\langle1-3,\,4-1,\,5-6\rangle\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(t=-2\) gives \(\langle1+6,\,4+2,\,5+12\rangle=\langle7,6,17\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      It starts at a point of \(L\) and its direction is a nonzero multiple of \(L\)'s direction.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition parallel lines

      Its direction \(\langle6,2,12\rangle=-2\langle-3,-1,-6\rangle\) is parallel to that of \(L\).
  2. 07

    Let \(\mathbf r_1(t)=\langle1,2,1\rangle+t\langle1,-1,1\rangle\) and \(\mathbf r_2(s)=\langle3,-1,1\rangle+s\langle a,b,-2\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      The lines \(\langle t,0,0\rangle\) and \(\langle0,s,1\rangle\) are skew.
    2. (b)
      Two lines in \(\mathbb R^3\) that are not parallel must intersect.
    3. (c)
      The lines are parallel exactly when \(a=-2\) and \(b=2\).
    4. (d)
      If \(b=4\), the lines intersect when \(a=-\frac{10}{3}\).
    5. (e)
      Every line has exactly one direction vector.
    Solution to problem 7

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      Their directions \(\langle1,0,0\rangle\) and \(\langle0,1,0\rangle\) are not parallel, and they never meet because one lies in \(z=0\) and the other in \(z=1\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition skew lines

      In three dimensions, nonparallel lines can miss each other; such lines are called skew.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition parallel lines

      The directions must be multiples: \(\langle a,b,-2\rangle=-2\langle1,-1,1\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      Set the coordinates equal: \(1+t=3+as\), \(2-t=-1+4s\), \(1+t=1-2s\). The last gives \(t=-2s\); the middle then gives \(s=\frac32\), \(t=-3\); the first gives \(-2=3+\frac32a\), so \(a=-\frac{10}{3}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Definition parametrization of a line

      Any nonzero multiple of a direction vector is also a direction vector.
  3. 08

    Let \(\mathbf r_1(t)=\mathbf v_1+t\mathbf w_1\) and \(\mathbf r_2(t)=\mathbf v_2+t\mathbf w_2\) parametrize lines \(L_1\) and \(L_2\), with \(\mathbf w_1,\mathbf w_2\neq\mathbf 0\).

    1. (a)
      If \(L_1=L_2\), then \(\mathbf v_1=\mathbf v_2\) and \(\mathbf w_1=\mathbf w_2\).
    2. (b)
      If \(\mathbf w_1\) is a multiple of \(\mathbf w_2\) and \(\mathbf v_1\) lies on \(L_2\), then \(L_1=L_2\).
    3. (c)
      If \(L_1\) is parallel to \(L_2\), then \(\mathbf w_1=\lambda\mathbf w_2\) for some scalar \(\lambda\).
    4. (d)
      If \(L_1=L_2\) and \(\mathbf w_1=\mathbf w_2\), then \(\mathbf v_1=\mathbf v_2\).
    5. (e)
      If \(L_1=L_2\), then \(\mathbf v_2-\mathbf v_1\) is a multiple of \(\mathbf w_1\).
    Solution to problem 8

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Definition parametrization of a line

      The same line can start at different points and use different direction vectors, e.g. \(t\langle1,1\rangle\) and \(\langle1,1\rangle+t\langle2,2\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(L_1\) is the line through a point of \(L_2\) in \(L_2\)'s direction, and there is only one such line.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition parallel lines

      Parallel lines have parallel direction vectors.
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Definition parametrization of a line

      \(\mathbf v_1\) and \(\mathbf v_2\) can be any two points of the line, e.g. \(t\langle1,0\rangle\) and \(\langle5,0\rangle+t\langle1,0\rangle\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf v_2\) lies on \(L_1\), so \(\mathbf v_2=\mathbf v_1+t_0\mathbf w_1\) for some \(t_0\).
  4. 09

    The following statements concern lines and planes.

    1. (a)
      The line \(\langle1+2t,\,-1+t,\,3t\rangle\) meets the plane \(x+y-z=2\) in exactly one point.
    2. (b)
      The direction of the line \(\langle1+2t,\,-1+t,\,3t\rangle\) is orthogonal to the normal of the plane \(x+y-z=2\).
    3. (c)
      The line \(\langle3t+2,\,1,\,-7t\rangle\) meets the plane \(2x-3y+z=5\) at \((11,1,-21)\).
    4. (d)
      The line through the origin with direction \(\langle1,1,2\rangle\) lies in the plane \(x+y-z=1\).
    5. (e)
      The line \(\langle1+2t,\,-1+t,\,3t\rangle\) lies in the plane \(x+y-z=2\).
    Solution to problem 9

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      Substituting gives \((1+2t)+(-1+t)-3t=0=2\), which has no solution. The line is parallel to the plane and misses it.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem line parallel to a plane

      \(\langle2,1,3\rangle\cdot\langle1,1,-1\rangle=2+1-3=0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      Substitute: \(2(3t+2)-3-7t=5\), so \(-t+1=5\) and \(t=-4\). The point is \((-10,1,28)\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      The origin does not satisfy \(x+y-z=1\). (The line does lie in the plane \(x+y-z=0\).)
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      Its point \((1,-1,0)\) gives \(1-1-0=0\neq2\).

Dot product, angles & projections

Problems 10–13
  1. 10

    Let \(\mathbf v=\langle1,3,-2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf w=\langle2,-1,4\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=-9\).
    2. (b)
      \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|^2=\|\mathbf v\|^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2\).
    3. (c)
      The angle between \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) is acute.
    4. (d)
      The angle between \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) is \(\cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{-9}{7\sqrt6}\right)\).
    5. (e)
      \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf v\).
    Solution to problem 10

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(2-3-8=-9\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Theorem Pythagorean theorem for vectors

      That holds only when \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=0\). Here \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|^2=\|\langle3,2,2\rangle\|^2=17\), while \(14+21=35\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem sign of the dot product

      \(\cos\theta=\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w}{\|\mathbf v\|\|\mathbf w\|}\) has the sign of \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=-9<0\), so the angle is obtuse.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Formula angle between vectors

      \(\|\mathbf v\|=\sqrt{14}\), \(\|\mathbf w\|=\sqrt{21}\), and \(\sqrt{14}\sqrt{21}=\sqrt{294}=7\sqrt6\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem properties of the dot product

      The dot product is commutative.
  2. 11

    Let \(\mathbf v=\langle1,-1,3\rangle\) and \(\mathbf w=\langle4,-2,1\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v=\frac37\langle4,-2,1\rangle\).
    2. (b)
      \(\mathbf v-\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v=\frac17\langle-5,-1,18\rangle\) is orthogonal to \(\mathbf w\).
    3. (c)
      \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf w\).
    4. (d)
      The scalar component of \(\mathbf v\) along \(\mathbf w\) is \(\frac{9}{\sqrt{21}}\).
    5. (e)
      The scalar component of \(\left\langle-2,\frac12,3\right\rangle\) along \(\langle1,2,2\rangle\) is \(\frac53\).
    Solution to problem 11

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Formula projection

      \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v=\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w}{\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf w}\mathbf w=\frac{9}{21}\mathbf w\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf v-\frac37\mathbf w=\frac17\langle7-12,\,-7+6,\,21-3\rangle\), and \(\langle-5,-1,18\rangle\cdot\langle4,-2,1\rangle=-20+2+18=0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Formula projection

      \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v\) is parallel to \(\mathbf w\), while \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf w=\frac{9}{11}\mathbf v\) is parallel to \(\mathbf v\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Formula scalar component

      \(\frac{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w}{\|\mathbf w\|}=\frac{9}{\sqrt{21}}\). (It is the signed length of \(\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf w}\mathbf v\).)
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Formula scalar component

      \(\frac{-2+1+6}{\sqrt{1+4+4}}=\frac53\).
  3. 12

    The following statements concern dot products and lengths.

    1. (a)
      If \(\|\mathbf v\|=\|\mathbf w\|=2\) and \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=1\), the parallelogram spanned by \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) has area \(\sqrt{15}\).
    2. (b)
      If \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|=\|\mathbf v\|=\|\mathbf w\|\neq0\), then the angle between \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) is \(\frac{2\pi}{3}\).
    3. (c)
      If \(\mathbf v\) and \(\mathbf w\) are orthogonal, then \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|^2=\|\mathbf v\|^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2\).
    4. (d)
      For all \(\mathbf v,\mathbf w\), \(\|\mathbf v-\mathbf w\|^2=\|\mathbf v\|^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2-2\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w\).
    5. (e)
      If \(\mathbf e,\mathbf f\) are unit vectors with \(\|\mathbf e+\mathbf f\|=\sqrt3\), then \(\|\mathbf e-4\mathbf f\|=\sqrt{13}\).
    Solution to problem 12

    Key: all five statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(\cos\theta=\frac14\), so \(\sin\theta=\frac{\sqrt{15}}{4}\) and the area is \(\|\mathbf v\|\|\mathbf w\|\sin\theta=\sqrt{15}\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      With \(a=\|\mathbf v\|\): \(a^2=a^2+2a^2\cos\theta+a^2\), so \(\cos\theta=-\frac12\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem Pythagorean theorem for vectors

      \(\|\mathbf v+\mathbf w\|^2=\|\mathbf v\|^2+2\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w+\|\mathbf w\|^2\), and \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w=0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Theorem properties of the dot product

      Expand \((\mathbf v-\mathbf w)\cdot(\mathbf v-\mathbf w)\) using distributivity and commutativity.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(3=1+2\,\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf f+1\) gives \(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf f=\frac12\). Then \(\|\mathbf e-4\mathbf f\|^2=1-8\cdot\frac12+16=13\).
  4. 13

    The following statements concern forces and work.

    1. (a)
      A wagon is pulled right by a force \(\mathbf F_1\) at \(30^\circ\) above the ground and left by a horizontal force \(\mathbf F_2\). If it does not move, \(\|\mathbf F_1\|=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\|\mathbf F_2\|\).
    2. (b)
      The work done by a constant force perpendicular to the displacement is \(\|\mathbf F\|\,\|\mathbf d\|\).
    3. (c)
      The work done by the constant force \(\langle3,4\rangle\) N moving an object along \(\langle5,0\rangle\) m is \(15\) J.
    4. (d)
      In the same situation, if \(\|\mathbf F_2\|=10\) N, then \(\|\mathbf F_1\|=20\) N.
    5. (e)
      In the same situation, the vertical component of \(\mathbf F_1\) has magnitude \(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\|\mathbf F_1\|\).
    Solution to problem 13

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      The horizontal components must cancel: \(\|\mathbf F_1\|\cos30^\circ=\|\mathbf F_2\|\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition work

      Work is \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d=\|\mathbf F\|\|\mathbf d\|\cos\theta\), which is \(0\) when \(\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition work

      Work is \(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf d=15+0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(\|\mathbf F_1\|=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\cdot10=\frac{20}{\sqrt3}\approx11.5\) N.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      The vertical component is \(\|\mathbf F_1\|\sin30^\circ=\frac12\|\mathbf F_1\|\); \(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\|\mathbf F_1\|\) is the horizontal component.

Cross & triple products

Problems 14–17
  1. 14

    Let \(\mathbf v=\langle1,2,4\rangle\), \(\mathbf w=\langle1,0,-3\rangle\), and \(\mathbf u=\langle6,-1,2\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(\mathbf w\times\mathbf v=\mathbf v\times\mathbf w\).
    2. (b)
      \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)=\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf w)\).
    3. (c)
      \(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w=\langle-6,7,-2\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      \((\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)\cdot\mathbf v=0\).
    5. (e)
      \(\mathbf v\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf w)=47\).
    Solution to problem 14

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Theorem anticommutativity

      \(\mathbf w\times\mathbf v=-(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)=\langle6,-7,2\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Theorem triple product and row swaps

      Both are \(3\times3\) determinants whose rows differ by swapping \(\mathbf u\) and \(\mathbf v\), which changes the sign: \(\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf v\times\mathbf w)=-47\).

      \(\mathbf r_1'\times\mathbf r_2+\mathbf r_1\times\mathbf r_2'=\langle-1,1,0\rangle+\langle4,-4,2\rangle\).
  5. 30

    The following statements concern derivatives and tangent lines.

    1. (a)
      The tangent line to \(\langle t^2,t^3\rangle\) at \(t=1\) is \(\langle1,1\rangle+s\langle2,3\rangle\).
    2. (b)
      \(\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf r(t)\|=\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\) for every differentiable \(\mathbf r\).
    3. (c)
      If \(\|\mathbf r(t)\|\) is constant, then \(\mathbf r(t)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)=0\).
    4. (d)
      For \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle\cos t,\sin t,1\rangle\), \(\mathbf r(t)\cdot\mathbf r'(t)=0\).
    5. (e)
      If \(\mathbf r'(t)=\mathbf 0\) for all \(t\), then \(\mathbf r\) is constant.
    Solution to problem 30

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Formula tangent line

      Point \(\mathbf r(1)=\langle1,1\rangle\), direction \(\mathbf r'(1)=\langle2,3\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      For \(\mathbf r=\langle\cos t,\sin t\rangle\), \(\|\mathbf r\|=1\) has derivative \(0\), but \(\|\mathbf r'\|=1\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem constant length

      Differentiate \(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf r=c\): \(2\,\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf r'=0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(\langle\cos t,\sin t,1\rangle\cdot\langle-\sin t,\cos t,0\rangle=0\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem zero derivative

      Each component has derivative \(0\), so each component is constant.

Integration & initial value problems

Problems 31–33
  1. 31

    Each statement concerns an integral of a vector-valued function.

    1. (a)
      \(\displaystyle\left\|\int_0^1\langle1,t\rangle\,dt\right\|=\sqrt5\).
    2. (b)
      \(\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\langle\sin\theta,\ \theta,\ \cos2\theta\rangle\,d\theta=\langle2,\ \pi^2,\ 0\rangle\).
    3. (c)
      \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\langle e^t,2t\rangle\,dt=\langle e,1\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\left\langle4t+3,\ t^2,\ -4t^3\right\rangle dt=\langle27,9,-81\rangle\).
    5. (e)
      \(\displaystyle\int_a^b\mathbf r'(t)\,dt=\mathbf r(b)-\mathbf r(a)\).
    Solution to problem 31

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(\int_0^1\langle1,t\rangle\,dt=\langle1,\frac12\rangle\), with length \(\frac{\sqrt5}{2}\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      The middle component is \(\left[\frac{\theta^2}{2}\right]_0^\pi=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(\int_0^1e^t\,dt=e-1\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(\left[2t^2+3t\right]_0^3=27\), \(\left[\frac{t^3}{3}\right]_0^3=9\), \(\left[-t^4\right]_0^3=-81\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem fundamental theorem of calculus

      Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus to each component.
  2. 32

    A particle is at \((1,1,0)\) at \(t=0\) and has velocity \(\mathbf v(t)=\langle1,t,2t^2\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      \(\left\langle1+t,\ 1+\frac{t^2}{2},\ \frac{2t^3}{3}\right\rangle+\mathbf C\) satisfies the initial condition for every constant vector \(\mathbf C\).
    2. (b)
      (A separate problem.) If \(\mathbf r''(t)=\langle4-16t,\ 12t^2-t\rangle\), \(\mathbf r'(0)=\langle1,0\rangle\), and \(\mathbf r(0)=\langle0,1\rangle\), then \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle t+2t^2-\frac{8t^3}{3},\ 1-\frac{t^3}{6}+t^4\right\rangle\).
    3. (c)
      Its position is \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle1+t,\ 1+\frac{t^2}{2},\ \frac{2t^3}{3}\right\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      (A separate problem.) The solution of \(\mathbf r'(t)=-\mathbf r(t)\) with \(\mathbf r(0)=\langle1,2\rangle\) is \(\mathbf r(t)=e^{-t}\langle1,2\rangle\).
    5. (e)
      Its position at \(t=2\) is \(\left\langle2,2,\frac{16}{3}\right\rangle\).
    Solution to problem 32

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      At \(t=0\) it equals \(\langle1,1,0\rangle+\mathbf C\), which is \(\langle1,1,0\rangle\) only when \(\mathbf C=\mathbf 0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf r'=\left\langle1+4t-8t^2,\ 4t^3-\frac{t^2}{2}\right\rangle\); integrate again and use \(\mathbf r(0)\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      Integrate \(\mathbf v\) and choose the constant so that \(\mathbf r(0)=\langle1,1,0\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      Each component solves \(x'=-x\), so \(x=x(0)e^{-t}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      That is \(\int_0^2\mathbf v\,dt\), the displacement. Adding the starting point gives \(\left\langle3,3,\frac{16}{3}\right\rangle\).
  3. 33

    A force \(\mathbf F(t)=\langle12t+4,\ 8-24t\rangle\) N acts on a \(2\)-kg mass that is at \((4,6)\) with velocity \(\langle2,3\rangle\) at \(t=0\).

    1. (a)
      The velocity at \(t=1\) is \(\langle7,1\rangle\).
    2. (b)
      The acceleration is \(\mathbf a(t)=\langle6t+2,\ 4-12t\rangle\).
    3. (c)
      The speed at \(t=1\) is \(8\).
    4. (d)
      The acceleration equals \(\mathbf F(t)\).
    5. (e)
      The position at \(t=2\) is \((20,4)\).
    Solution to problem 33

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf v(t)=\langle2+2t+3t^2,\ 3+4t-6t^2\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem Newton’s second law

      \(\mathbf F=m\mathbf a\), so \(\mathbf a=\frac12\mathbf F\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(\|\langle7,1\rangle\|=\sqrt{50}=5\sqrt2\). \(8=7+1\) adds components instead of taking the length.
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Theorem Newton’s second law

      \(\mathbf a=\mathbf F/m\), and \(m=2\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle4+2t+t^2+t^3,\ 6+3t+2t^2-2t^3\rangle\); at \(t=2\): \(\langle20,4\rangle\).

Arc length, motion & unit tangent

Problems 34–38
  1. 34

    Let \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle\sin2t,\ \cos2t,\ 3t-1\rangle\) for \(1\le t\le3\).

    1. (a)
      The length of the path is \(3\sqrt{13}\).
    2. (b)
      The speed is constant, equal to \(\sqrt{13}\).
    3. (c)
      \(\mathbf T(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}\langle2\cos2t,\ -2\sin2t,\ 3\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      The length of the path is \(\displaystyle\int_1^3\|\mathbf r(t)\|\,dt\).
    5. (e)
      The path is a circle.
    Solution to problem 34

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Formula arc length

      \(\displaystyle\int_1^3\sqrt{13}\,dt=2\sqrt{13}\). The interval has length \(2\), not \(3\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf r'=\langle2\cos2t,-2\sin2t,3\rangle\) and \(\|\mathbf r'\|^2=4+9\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition unit tangent vector

      \(\mathbf T=\frac{\mathbf r'}{\|\mathbf r'\|}\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Formula arc length

      Arc length integrates the speed \(\|\mathbf r'(t)\|\), not \(\|\mathbf r(t)\|\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      \(z=3t-1\) increases while \((x,y)\) goes around the unit circle: it is a helix.
  2. 35

    A helix of height \(20\) cm makes four full turns around a circle of radius \(5\) cm: \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle5\cos t,\ 5\sin t,\ \frac{5}{2\pi}t\right\rangle\), \(0\le t\le8\pi\).

    1. (a)
      For an arc length parametrization \(\mathbf r(s)\), \(\|\mathbf r'(s)\|=1\) for all \(s\).
    2. (b)
      One full turn of the helix has length \(10\pi\) cm.
    3. (c)
      Its total length is \(20\sqrt{4\pi^2+1}\) cm.
    4. (d)
      The helix rises \(5\) cm per full turn.
    5. (e)
      An arc length parametrization is \(\mathbf r(s/c)\), where \(c\) is the (constant) speed.
    Solution to problem 35

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition arc length parametrization

      Moving one unit of parameter covers one unit of length.
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      \(10\pi\) is the circumference of the circle, which ignores the rise. One turn has length \(5\sqrt{4\pi^2+1}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Formula arc length

      The speed is \(\sqrt{25+\frac{25}{4\pi^2}}=\frac{5}{2\pi}\sqrt{4\pi^2+1}\); multiply by \(8\pi\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      One turn is \(\Delta t=2\pi\), and \(\frac{5}{2\pi}\cdot2\pi=5\). Four turns give \(20\) cm.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition arc length parametrization

      Then \(s=ct\) measures distance traveled, so \(\left\|\frac{d}{ds}\mathbf r(s/c)\right\|=1\).
  3. 36

    A particle has trajectory \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle t,\ e^{t-3},\ e^{4-t}\right\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      The square of its speed is \(1+e^{2t-6}+e^{8-2t}\).
    2. (b)
      Its velocity and acceleration are orthogonal for all \(t\).
    3. (c)
      For any path with constant speed, \(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a=0\).
    4. (d)
      Its minimum speed is \(1+2e\).
    5. (e)
      Its minimum speed occurs at \(t=3\).
    Solution to problem 36

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(\mathbf r'=\langle1,e^{t-3},-e^{4-t}\rangle\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Theorem speed and acceleration

      \(\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf v\|^2=2\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a\), which is \(0\) only at \(t=\frac72\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem speed and acceleration

      \(\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathbf v\|^2=2\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf a\), and the left side is \(0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      At \(t=\frac72\) the squared speed is \(1+e+e=1+2e\), so the speed is \(\sqrt{1+2e}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      Setting the derivative \(2e^{2t-6}-2e^{8-2t}\) to \(0\) gives \(2t-6=8-2t\), so \(t=\frac72\).
  4. 37

    A mouse runs counterclockwise around a circle of radius \(0.6\) m at speed \(0.3\) m/s on the floor of an elevator rising at a constant \(12\) m/s. Take \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle0.6\cos\frac t2,\ 0.6\sin\frac t2,\ 12t\right\rangle\).

    1. (a)
      The acceleration has a vertical component of \(12\) m/s\(^2\).
    2. (b)
      The acceleration points away from the axis of the circle.
    3. (c)
      The mouse's speed (relative to the ground) is \(0.3\) m/s.
    4. (d)
      The magnitude of the acceleration is \(0.5\) m/s\(^2\).
    5. (e)
      The mouse's angular speed is \(\frac12\) rad/s.
    Solution to problem 37

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      The vertical velocity is constant, so its derivative is \(0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      \(\mathbf a\) is a negative multiple of \(\langle\cos\frac t2,\sin\frac t2,0\rangle\), so it points toward the axis (centripetal).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(\|\mathbf r'\|=\sqrt{0.3^2+12^2}\approx12\) m/s; \(0.3\) m/s is its speed relative to the elevator floor.
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(\mathbf a=-0.15\left\langle\cos\frac t2,\sin\frac t2,0\right\rangle\), so \(\|\mathbf a\|=\frac{v^2}{R}=0.15\) m/s\(^2\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\omega=\frac{v}{R}=\frac{0.3}{0.6}\).
  5. 38

    The following statements concern unit tangent vectors and tangent lines.

    1. (a)
      The tangent line to \(\left\langle-\frac{100}{t^2},\ 7-t,\ 40-t^2\right\rangle\) at \(t=1\) meets the \(yz\)-plane at \((0,6,39)\).
    2. (b)
      \(\mathbf T(t)\) is a unit vector pointing in the direction of \(\mathbf r'(t)\) (when \(\mathbf r'(t)\neq\mathbf 0\)).
    3. (c)
      For \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle\sin t,\ t,\ \cos t\rangle\), \(\mathbf T(\pi)=\langle-1,1,0\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      \(\mathbf T(t)=\mathbf r'(t)\) for every path.
    5. (e)
      For \(\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle t^2,\ \tan^{-1}t,\ t\right\rangle\), \(\mathbf T(1)=\left\langle2,\frac12,1\right\rangle\).
    Solution to problem 38

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      The line is \(\langle-100,6,39\rangle+s\langle200,-1,-2\rangle\). It reaches \(x=0\) at \(s=\frac12\), the point \(\left(0,\frac{11}{2},38\right)\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Definition unit tangent vector

      \(\mathbf T=\frac{\mathbf r'}{\|\mathbf r'\|}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Definition unit tangent vector

      \(\mathbf r'(\pi)=\langle-1,1,0\rangle\) has length \(\sqrt2\), so \(\mathbf T(\pi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\langle-1,1,0\rangle\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Definition unit tangent vector

      Only when the speed is \(1\); in general \(\mathbf r'\) must be divided by its length.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Definition unit tangent vector

      \(\mathbf r'(1)=\langle2,\frac12,1\rangle\) has length \(\frac{\sqrt{21}}{2}\), so \(\mathbf T(1)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{21}}\langle4,1,2\rangle\).

Cylindrical & spherical coordinates

Problems 39–40
  1. 39

    Cylindrical coordinates are \((r,\theta,z)\) and spherical coordinates are \((\rho,\theta,\phi)\), with \(\phi\) measured from the positive \(z\)-axis.

    1. (a)
      The cylindrical point \(\left(3,\frac{\pi}{6},4\right)\) has spherical \(\phi=\tan^{-1}\frac43\).
    2. (b)
      The solid cylinder \(x^2+y^2\le4\) is \(\rho\le2\) in spherical coordinates.
    3. (c)
      The spherical point \((\rho,\theta,\phi)=\left(3,\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{\pi}{3}\right)\) has cylindrical coordinates \(\left(\frac{3\sqrt3}{2},\frac{\pi}{6},\frac32\right)\).
    4. (d)
      \((x,y,z)=(3,4,-1)\) has cylindrical coordinates \(\left(5,\tan^{-1}\frac43,-1\right)\).
    5. (e)
      \((3,4,-1)\) has \(\rho=\sqrt{26}\).
    Solution to problem 39

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Formula spherical conversion

      \(\tan\phi=\frac rz=\frac34\), so \(\phi=\tan^{-1}\frac34\) (and \(\rho=5\)).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Formula spherical conversion

      \(\rho\le2\) is a ball. The cylinder is \(r\le2\), which in spherical coordinates is \(\rho\sin\phi\le2\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Formula spherical to cylindrical

      \(r=\rho\sin\phi=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\) and \(z=\rho\cos\phi=\frac32\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Formula cylindrical conversion

      \(r=\sqrt{9+16}=5\), \(\tan\theta=\frac43\) with \(x,y>0\), and \(z\) is unchanged.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Formula spherical conversion

      \(\rho=\sqrt{9+16+1}\).
  2. 40

    The following statements concern equations in cylindrical and spherical coordinates.

    1. (a)
      The spherical equation \(\phi=\frac{\pi}{4}\) is a cone.
    2. (b)
      The cylindrical equation \(z=2r\cos\theta\) is the plane \(z=2x\).
    3. (c)
      The cylindrical equation \(r=3\) is a circular cylinder.
    4. (d)
      \(x^2+y^2-z^2=2(x+y)\) is \(r^2-z^2=2r(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)\) in cylindrical coordinates.
    5. (e)
      \(r^2\left(1-2\sin^2\theta\right)+z^2=1\) is a hyperboloid of one sheet.
    Solution to problem 40

    Key: all five statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Formula spherical conversion

      \(\tan\phi=\frac rz=1\) gives \(z=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Formula cylindrical conversion

      \(x=r\cos\theta\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Formula cylindrical conversion

      \(x^2+y^2=9\) with \(z\) free.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Formula cylindrical conversion

      \(x^2+y^2=r^2\), \(x=r\cos\theta\), \(y=r\sin\theta\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Formula cylindrical conversion

      \(r^2-2r^2\sin^2\theta=x^2+y^2-2y^2=x^2-y^2\), so the surface is \(x^2-y^2+z^2=1\).
