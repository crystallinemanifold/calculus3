Directions

Read each statement carefully. Circle every true statement and cross out every false one. A problem may have one true statement, several, or all five.

Mark every statement. A statement left unmarked counts as wrong.

Use ○ to circle and ✕ to cross out; click again to undo. The exam has six problems in 35 minutes, so aim for about six minutes per problem. Do a whole problem before checking it.

How to approach each statement

Treat every statement under a problem as its own small question, and do not decide it by how it looks. Each statement is settled by exactly one of four things:

  • Definition such as a partial derivative, the gradient, a level curve, a critical point, or continuity;
  • Theorem or property, such as the chain rule, Clairaut’s theorem, the second derivative test, the extreme value theorem, or the Lagrange condition;
  • Formula such as the tangent plane, the linear approximation, \(D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf u\), or \(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\);
  • Calculation to carry out: differentiate, solve for critical points, evaluate and compare.

Before you circle or cross out, name which one applies, then use it. If it is a calculation, do the calculation.

Example. Problem 19(e) claims the directional derivative at \(P\) in the direction of \(\mathbf v=\langle2,1\rangle\) is \(-\frac{54}{\sqrt5}\). That calls for a formula: \(D_{\mathbf u}f(P)=\nabla f(P)\cdot\mathbf u\), where \(\mathbf u\) is a unit vector. So first normalize \(\mathbf v\) to \(\mathbf u=\frac{1}{\sqrt5}\langle2,1\rangle\), then calculate: \(\langle27,-108\rangle\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt5}\langle2,1\rangle=-\frac{54}{\sqrt5}\). The statement is true. (A statement giving \(-54\) would be false: it skips the normalization.)

Domains, graphs & level curves

Problems 1–5
  1. 01

    Let \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{\sqrt{x^2-y^2}}{x+3}\).

    1. (a)
      The domain of \(f\) is \(\{(x,y)\mid |y|\le|x|,\ x\neq-3\}\).
    2. (b)
      \((1,2)\) is in the domain of \(f\).
    3. (c)
      \(f(3,1)=\dfrac{\sqrt2}{3}\).
    4. (d)
      \(f(-5,-3)=-2\).
    5. (e)
      \(f\!\left(-\frac32,0\right)=1\).
    Solution to problem 1

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition domain

      The square root needs \(x^2-y^2\ge0\), i.e. \(|y|\le|x|\), and the denominator needs \(x\neq-3\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition domain

      \(1^2-2^2=-3<0\), so the square root is undefined.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(\dfrac{\sqrt{9-1}}{6}=\dfrac{2\sqrt2}{6}\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(\dfrac{\sqrt{25-9}}{-2}=\dfrac{4}{-2}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\dfrac{\sqrt{9/4}}{3/2}=\dfrac{3/2}{3/2}=1\).
  2. 02

    The following statements concern domains and ranges.

    1. (a)
      The range of \(f(x,y,z)=\sqrt{x-y}+\sqrt{y-z}\) is \([0,\infty)\).
    2. (b)
      The domain of \(f(x,y)=\ln(4x^2-y)\) is the region \(y<4x^2\).
    3. (c)
      The domain of \(f(x,y,z)=\sqrt{x-y}+\sqrt{y-z}\) is \(\{x\ge y\ge z\}\).
    4. (d)
      The domain of \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{1}{x^2+y^2}\) is all of \(\mathbb R^2\).
    5. (e)
      The range of \(f(x,y)=\ln(4x^2-y)\) is \((0,\infty)\).
    Solution to problem 2

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition range

      Each term is \(\ge0\), and \(f(t,0,0)=\sqrt t\) takes every nonnegative value.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Definition domain

      \(\ln\) needs a positive argument.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition domain

      Both square roots need nonnegative arguments: \(x-y\ge0\) and \(y-z\ge0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Definition domain

      The origin must be excluded.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Definition range

      \(4x^2-y\) takes every positive value, so \(\ln(4x^2-y)\) takes every real value. The range is \(\mathbb R\).
  3. 03

    Each statement describes the level curves \(f(x,y)=c\).

    1. (a)
      The level curve \(f=0\) of \(f(x,y)=3x^2-4y^2\) is a single point.
    2. (b)
      For \(c\neq0\), the level curves of \(f(x,y)=3x^2-4y^2\) are hyperbolas.
    3. (c)
      The level curves of \(f(x,y)=\ln(4x-y)\) are circles.
    4. (d)
      The level curves of \(f(x,y)=e^{4x-y}\) are lines of slope \(4\).
    5. (e)
      The level curves of \(f(x,y)=x+y^2\) are parabolas.
    Solution to problem 3

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(3x^2=4y^2\) gives the pair of lines \(y=\pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2}x\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Definition level curve

      \(3x^2-4y^2=c\) is a hyperbola when \(c\neq0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Definition level curve

      \(\ln(4x-y)=c\) means \(4x-y=e^c\): lines of slope \(4\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Definition level curve

      For \(c>0\), \(e^{4x-y}=c\) means \(y=4x-\ln c\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition level curve

      \(x=c-y^2\): parabolas opening toward negative \(x\).
  4. 04

    Let \(f(x,y)=x^2-y+1\).

    1. (a)
      The point \((1,2,0)\) lies on the graph of \(f\).
    2. (b)
      The horizontal traces (level curves) are lines.
    3. (c)
      The vertical traces in the planes \(y=c\) are parabolas.
    4. (d)
      The graph of \(f\) is a plane.
    5. (e)
      The vertical traces of the graph in the planes \(x=c\) are lines.
    Solution to problem 4

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(f(1,2)=1-2+1=0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition level curve

      \(x^2-y+1=c\) gives \(y=x^2+1-c\), a parabola.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition trace

      \(z=x^2+1-c\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Definition graph

      \(f\) is not linear because of the \(x^2\) term; the graph is a parabolic cylinder tilted along \(y\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition trace

      \(z=c^2+1-y\) is linear in \(y\).
  5. 05

    The following statements concern contour maps.

    1. (a)
      Two different level curves of the same function can cross.
    2. (b)
      The level curves of \(f(x,y)=e^{xy}\) are circles.
    3. (c)
      The level curves of \(f(x,y)=xy\) are lines.
    4. (d)
      Where the level curves (for equally spaced values) are close together, \(\|\nabla f\|\) is large.
    5. (e)
      \(\nabla f(P)\) is tangent to the level curve through \(P\).
    Solution to problem 5

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Definition level curve

      A crossing point would have two different values of \(f\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Definition level curve

      \(e^{xy}=c\) means \(xy=\ln c\): the same hyperbolas as for \(xy\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Definition level curve

      For \(c\neq0\), \(xy=c\) is a hyperbola; only \(c=0\) gives lines (the axes).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Theorem gradient and level curves

      The function changes by the same amount over a shorter distance, so the rate of change is larger.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Theorem gradient and level curves

      \(\nabla f(P)\) is perpendicular (normal) to the level curve through \(P\).

Limits & continuity

Problems 6–9
  1. 06

    Each statement claims the value of a limit.

    1. (a)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(1,-3)}\ln(3x+y)=0\).
    2. (b)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(1,-3)}\left(xy+y^2\right)=6\).
    3. (c)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,2)}\frac{(e^x-1)(e^y-1)}{x}=e^2-1\).
    4. (d)
      The limit of a polynomial at any point can be found by substitution.
    5. (e)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(1,-3)}(2x+y)e^{-x+y}=e^{-4}\).
    Solution to problem 6

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(3x+y\to0^{\pm}\), and \(\ln\) is undefined at \(0\) (and for negative inputs). The limit does not exist.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem continuity of polynomials

      Polynomials are continuous, so substitute: \(-3+9=6\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      Split it: \(\frac{e^x-1}{x}\to1\) and \(e^y-1\to e^2-1\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Theorem continuity of polynomials

      Polynomials in \(x\) and \(y\) are continuous everywhere.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Theorem continuity

      The function is continuous, so substitute: \((2-3)e^{-1-3}=-e^{-4}\). The sign is wrong.
  2. 07

    Let \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{xy}{x^2+y^2}\) for \((x,y)\neq(0,0)\).

    1. (a)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{xy+xy^2}{x^2+y^2}\) does not exist.
    2. (b)
      Along the \(x\)-axis, \(f(x,y)\to0\) as \((x,y)\to(0,0)\).
    3. (c)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}f(x,y)=0\).
    4. (d)
      Along the line \(y=x\), \(f(x,y)\to\frac12\).
    5. (e)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{x^2y}{x^2+y^2}=0\).
    Solution to problem 7

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Theorem two-path test

      Along \(y=mx\) the expression tends to \(\frac{m}{1+m^2}\), which depends on \(m\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(f(x,0)=0\) for \(x\neq0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem two-path test

      Two paths give different values (\(0\) and \(\tfrac12\)), so the limit does not exist.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(f(x,x)=\frac{x^2}{2x^2}=\frac12\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem squeeze theorem

      \(\frac{x^2}{x^2+y^2}\le1\), so \(\left|\frac{x^2y}{x^2+y^2}\right|\le|y|\to0\).
  3. 08

    The following statements concern limits along paths and continuity.

    1. (a)
      If \(f\) has the same limit along two different paths to \(P\), then the limit at \(P\) exists.
    2. (b)
      For \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2}\), \(f\to0\) along the parabola \(y=x^2\).
    3. (c)
      If \(f(x,y)\to0\) along every line through the origin, then \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}f=0\).
    4. (d)
      \(f(x,y)=\dfrac{e^{x-y}}{x+1}\) is continuous everywhere.
    5. (e)
      If \(f\) is continuous at \(P\), then \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to P}f(x,y)=f(P)\).
    Solution to problem 8

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Theorem two-path test

      Agreement along some paths is not enough; the limit must be the same along every path. (Disagreement along two paths does prove the limit fails.)
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      \(f(x,x^2)=\frac{x^4}{2x^4}=\frac12\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem two-path test

      Lines are not all the paths. \(f=\frac{x^2y}{x^4+y^2}\) tends to \(0\) along every line, but equals \(\frac12\) on the parabola \(y=x^2\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Theorem continuity

      It is undefined on the line \(x=-1\). It is continuous at every point with \(x\neq-1\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition continuity

      This is the definition of continuity at \(P\).
  4. 09

    Each statement concerns a limit at the origin.

    1. (a)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{\sin(x^2+y^2)}{x^2+y^2}=1\).
    2. (b)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{x^3+y^3}{x^2+y^2}=0\).
    3. (c)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{xy}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}=0\).
    4. (d)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{x^2-y^2}{x^2+y^2}=1\).
    5. (e)
      \(\displaystyle\lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}\frac{x+y}{x-y}=1\).
    Solution to problem 9

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      With \(u=x^2+y^2\to0^+\), \(\frac{\sin u}{u}\to1\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem squeeze theorem

      In polar form the expression is \(r(\cos^3\theta+\sin^3\theta)\), whose absolute value is at most \(2r\to0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem squeeze theorem

      \(|xy|\le x^2+y^2\), so the absolute value is at most \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\to0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Theorem two-path test

      Along the \(x\)-axis the value is \(1\); along the \(y\)-axis it is \(-1\). The limit does not exist.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Theorem two-path test

      Along \(y=0\) the value is \(1\); along \(x=0\) it is \(-1\). The limit does not exist.

Partial derivatives

Problems 10–13
  1. 10

    Each statement claims a partial derivative.

    1. (a)
      If \(f(x,y)=\sqrt{7x+y^2}\), then \(f_x(1,3)=\frac78\).
    2. (b)
      If \(f(x,y)=\sqrt{7x+y^2}\), then \(f_y(1,3)=\frac38\).
    3. (c)
      If \(f(x,y)=\ln(x^2+xy^2)\), then \(f_x=\dfrac{2x+y^2}{x^2+xy^2}\).
    4. (d)
      If \(f(x,y)=2x+y^2\), then \(f_x=2\) and \(f_y=y^2\).
    5. (e)
      If \(f(x,y)=4xy^3\), then \(f_y=12xy^2\).
    Solution to problem 10

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=\frac{7}{2\sqrt{7x+y^2}}\), and \(\sqrt{7+9}=4\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      \(f_y=\frac{2y}{2\sqrt{7x+y^2}}=\frac{3}{4}\) at \((1,3)\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      Chain rule: the derivative of the inside with respect to \(x\) is \(2x+y^2\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Definition partial derivative

      \(f_y\) is the derivative of \(y^2\) in \(y\): \(f_y=2y\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Definition partial derivative

      Treat \(x\) as a constant.
  2. 11

    The following statements concern partial derivatives.

    1. (a)
      \(f_x(a,b)\) is the slope of the trace of the graph in the plane \(x=a\).
    2. (b)
      If \(f_{xy}\) and \(f_{yx}\) are continuous, then \(f_{xy}=f_{yx}\).
    3. (c)
      If \(f(x,y)=\sin(xy)e^{-x-y}\), then \(f_x=y\cos(xy)e^{-x-y}\).
    4. (d)
      If \(f(x,y,z)=y\sin(x+z)\), then \(f_{xxyz}=-\cos(x+z)\).
    5. (e)
      If \(f(x,y)=x^3y^2\), then \(f_{xy}=6x^2y^2\).
    Solution to problem 11

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Definition partial derivative

      \(f_x\) varies \(x\) with \(y\) held fixed, so it is the slope of the trace in the plane \(y=b\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem Clairaut’s theorem

      This is Clairaut's theorem on mixed partial derivatives.
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem product rule

      Both factors depend on \(x\), so use the product rule: \(f_x=e^{-x-y}\big(y\cos(xy)-\sin(xy)\big)\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=y\cos(x+z)\), \(f_{xx}=-y\sin(x+z)\), \(f_{xxy}=-\sin(x+z)\), \(f_{xxyz}=-\cos(x+z)\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      \(f_x=3x^2y^2\), so \(f_{xy}=6x^2y\).
  3. 12

    Each statement claims that a function satisfies a partial differential equation.

    1. (a)
      \(u(t,x)=\sin(x-ct)\) satisfies \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\).
    2. (b)
      \(f(x,y)=x^2-y^2\) satisfies \(f_{xx}+f_{yy}=0\).
    3. (c)
      \(f(x,y)=x^2+y^2\) satisfies \(f_{xx}+f_{yy}=0\).
    4. (d)
      \(u(t,x)=e^{-t}\sin(2x)\) satisfies \(u_t=u_{xx}\).
    5. (e)
      \(u(t,x)=\sin(ct)\sin x\) satisfies \(u_{tt}=c^2u_{xx}\).
    Solution to problem 12

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(u_{tt}=-c^2\sin(x-ct)\) and \(u_{xx}=-\sin(x-ct)\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(2+(-2)=0\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(2+2=4\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(u_t=-e^{-t}\sin2x\), but \(u_{xx}=-4e^{-t}\sin2x\). (\(e^{-4t}\sin2x\) would work.)
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(u_{tt}=-c^2\sin(ct)\sin x\) and \(u_{xx}=-\sin(ct)\sin x\).
  4. 13

    Let \(f(x,y)=x^2y-3y^2\).

    1. (a)
      \(f_{xx}=2y\).
    2. (b)
      \(f_{yy}=-6\).
    3. (c)
      With \(y=1\) held fixed, \(f\) is increasing in \(x\) at \((2,1)\).
    4. (d)
      \(f_x(2,1)=4\).
    5. (e)
      \(f_y(2,1)=-2\).
    Solution to problem 13

    Key: all five statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      Differentiate \(2xy\) in \(x\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      Differentiate \(x^2-6y\) in \(y\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition partial derivative

      \(f_x(2,1)=4>0\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=2xy\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(f_y=x^2-6y=4-6\).

Tangent planes & linear approximation

Problems 14–18
  1. 14

    Let \(f(x,y)=xy^2-xy+3x^3y\) and \(P=(1,3)\).

    1. (a)
      The tangent plane at \(P\) is \(z=33x+8y\).
    2. (b)
      The tangent plane at \(P\) is \(z=15+33(x-1)+8(y-3)\).
    3. (c)
      \(f_x(1,3)=33\).
    4. (d)
      \(f(1,3)=15\).
    5. (e)
      \(\langle33,8,1\rangle\) is normal to the graph of \(f\) at \((1,3,15)\).
    Solution to problem 14

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Formula tangent plane

      This plane passes through \((1,3,57)\), not \((1,3,15)\). The tangent plane must pass through the point of tangency.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Formula tangent plane

      \(f_y=2xy-x+3x^3=6-1+3=8\), and the plane is \(z=f(P)+f_x(P)(x-1)+f_y(P)(y-3)\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=y^2-y+9x^2y=9-3+27\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(9-3+9\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Formula normal to a graph

      Writing the graph as \(f(x,y)-z=0\), a normal is \(\langle f_x,f_y,-1\rangle=\langle33,8,-1\rangle\). \(\langle33,8,1\rangle\) is not a multiple of it.
  2. 15

    The following statements concern linear approximation.

    1. (a)
      With the tangent plane \(z=2x-y-1\) at \((5,3)\), \(f(5.2,2.9)\approx6.3\).
    2. (b)
      The linear approximation \(L(x,y)\) equals \(f(x,y)\) exactly for every function \(f\).
    3. (c)
      With the same data, \(f(3.88,4.03)\approx3.15\).
    4. (d)
      If \(z=2x-y-1\) is the tangent plane to \(z=f(x,y)\) at \((5,3)\), then \(f(5,3)=6\).
    5. (e)
      If \(f(4,4)=3\) and \(f_x(4,4)=f_y(4,4)=-1\), the linear approximation gives \(f(4.1,4)\approx2.9\).
    Solution to problem 15

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Formula linear approximation

      \(f_x=2\), \(f_y=-1\): \(6+2(0.2)-1(-0.1)=6.5\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Formula linear approximation

      It is exact only when \(f\) is linear; otherwise it is accurate only near the base point.
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Formula linear approximation

      \(3+(-1)(-0.12)+(-1)(0.03)=3.09\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Formula tangent plane

      The tangent plane passes through the point on the graph: \(2\cdot5-3-1=6\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Formula linear approximation

      \(3+(-1)(0.1)+(-1)(0)\).
  3. 16

    Let \(f(x,y,z)=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z}\) near the point \((3,4,11)\).

    1. (a)
      \(df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dy+f_z\,dz\).
    2. (b)
      The linear approximation gives \(\sqrt{3.1^2+3.9^2+11.6}\approx6.42\).
    3. (c)
      \(\nabla f(3,4,11)=\left\langle\frac12,\frac23,\frac1{12}\right\rangle\).
    4. (d)
      \(f(3,4,11)=36\).
    5. (e)
      The linear approximation gives \(\sqrt{3.1^2+3.9^2+11.6}\approx6.033\).
    Solution to problem 16

    Key: three statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Definition differential

      This is the total differential.
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Formula linear approximation

      That adds the input changes directly to \(f\). Each change must be weighted by the matching partial derivative.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=\frac{x}{f}\), \(f_y=\frac{y}{f}\), \(f_z=\frac{1}{2f}\), with \(f=6\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(f(3,4,11)=\sqrt{9+16+11}=\sqrt{36}=6\); \(36\) forgets the square root.

      With \(x=54^{-1/3}\), \(y=3x^2\): \(72xy=216x^3=216/54=4\), so \(D=4-1\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Theorem second derivative test

      There \(D=72xy-1=3>0\) and \(f_{xx}=6x>0\): a local minimum.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem second derivative test

      The point could be a maximum, minimum, or saddle.
  4. 32

    The following statements concern critical points.

    1. (a)
      If a differentiable \(f\) has a local minimum at an interior point \(P\), then \(\nabla f(P)=\mathbf 0\).
    2. (b)
      \(\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\) is a local minimum of \(f(x,y)=e^{x+y}-xe^{2y}\).
    3. (c)
      The only critical point of \(f(x,y)=e^{x+y}-xe^{2y}\) is \(\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\).
    4. (d)
      \(f(x,y)=(x+2y)e^{xy}\) has no critical points.
    5. (e)
      \(\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\) is a saddle point of \(f(x,y)=e^{x+y}-xe^{2y}\).
    Solution to problem 32

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Theorem Fermat’s theorem

      Each one-variable slice through \(P\) has a minimum there, so each partial derivative is \(0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Theorem second derivative test

      \(D<0\), so it is a saddle point.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Definition critical point

      \(f_x=e^{x+y}-e^{2y}=0\) gives \(x=y\); \(f_y=e^{x+y}-2xe^{2y}=e^{2y}(1-2x)=0\) gives \(x=\frac12\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(f_x=e^{xy}\big(1+y(x+2y)\big)\) and \(f_y=e^{xy}\big(2+x(x+2y)\big)\). Both zero would give \(y(x+2y)=-1\) and \(x(x+2y)=-2\), so \(x=2y\) and then \(4y^2=-1\), impossible.
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem second derivative test

      There \(f_{xx}=e\), \(f_{xy}=e-2e=-e\), \(f_{yy}=e-2e=-e\), so \(D=-e^2-e^2<0\).
  5. 33

    The following statements concern the second derivative test.

    1. (a)
      If \(D<0\) at a critical point, it is a local minimum.
    2. (b)
      The discriminant is \(D=f_{xx}f_{yy}-f_{xy}^2\).
    3. (c)
      If \(D>0\) and \(f_{xx}<0\) at a critical point, it is a local minimum.
    4. (d)
      \(f(x,y)=x^2+y^4\) has \(D=0\) at the origin, yet the origin is a local minimum.
    5. (e)
      It is possible to have \(D>0\) and \(f_{xx}=0\) at the same point.
    Solution to problem 33

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Theorem second derivative test

      \(D<0\) means a saddle point.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Formula discriminant

      This is the determinant of the matrix of second partial derivatives.
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem second derivative test

      \(f_{xx}<0\) means concave down: a local maximum.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Theorem second derivative test

      \(f_{yy}=12y^2=0\) at the origin, so \(D=0\); but \(f\ge0=f(0,0)\) everywhere.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Formula discriminant

      If \(f_{xx}=0\), then \(D=-f_{xy}^2\le0\).

Global extrema on closed, bounded sets

Problems 34–36
  1. 34

    Let \(f(x,y)=x^3-xy-y^2+y\) on the square \([0,1]\times[0,1]\).

    1. (a)
      The global maximum is \(f\!\left(\frac13,\frac13\right)=\frac{4}{27}\).
    2. (b)
      The global minimum is \(0\).
    3. (c)
      \(f\) is guaranteed to have a global maximum and minimum on the square.
    4. (d)
      The only critical point inside the square is \(\left(\frac13,\frac13\right)\).
    5. (e)
      Global extrema on a closed, bounded set always occur at interior critical points.
    Solution to problem 34

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      On the edge \(y=0\), \(f=x^3\) reaches \(1\) at \((1,0)\). The global maximum is \(1\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      On the edge \(y=1\), \(f=x^3-x\), which reaches \(-\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\) at \(x=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Theorem extreme value theorem

      \(f\) is continuous and the square is closed and bounded.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Definition critical point

      \(f_x=3x^2-y=0\), \(f_y=-x-2y+1=0\) give \(6x^2+x-1=0\), so \(x=\frac13\) (the root \(-\frac12\) is outside).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Theorem extreme value theorem

      They can occur on the boundary, as they do here.
  2. 35

    Let \(f(x,y)=2xy-x-y\) on the region \(D=\{(x,y)\mid x^2\le y\le4\}\).

    1. (a)
      The critical point \(\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\) lies in \(D\).
    2. (b)
      \(D\) is closed and bounded.
    3. (c)
      \(f\!\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)\) is the global minimum of \(f\) on \(D\).
    4. (d)
      The global maximum of \(f\) on \(D\) is \(10\), at \((2,4)\).
    5. (e)
      The global minimum of \(f\) on \(D\) is \(-18\), at \((-2,4)\).
    Solution to problem 35

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      \(\left(\frac12\right)^2=\frac14\le\frac12\le4\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem extreme value theorem

      It includes its boundary curves and fits inside a box.
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(f\!\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)=-\frac12\), but \(f(-2,4)=-18\) is smaller.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      On \(y=4\), \(f=7x-4\) for \(-2\le x\le2\); on \(y=x^2\), \(f=2x^3-x^2-x\). Comparing all candidates, the largest value is \(f(2,4)=10\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(f(-2,4)=-16+2-4=-18\), the smallest candidate value.
  3. 36

    The following statements concern global extrema.

    1. (a)
      \(f(x,y)=x+y\) has no maximum on the open disk \(x^2+y^2<1\).
    2. (b)
      \(f(x,y)=x^2+y^2\) has maximum value \(2\) on the closed unit disk.
    3. (c)
      \(f(x,y)=x^2-y^2\) has maximum value \(2\) on the closed unit disk.
    4. (d)
      A continuous function on \(\mathbb R^2\) always has a global maximum.
    5. (e)
      On a closed, bounded region, the only candidates for global extrema are the interior critical points.
    Solution to problem 36

    Key: one statement is true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Theorem extreme value theorem

      Values approach \(\sqrt2\) near the boundary but never reach it; the extreme value theorem needs a closed set.
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      The maximum is \(1\), attained on the boundary circle.
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      On the disk \(x^2-y^2\le x^2\le1\); the maximum is \(1\), at \((\pm1,0)\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Theorem extreme value theorem

      \(\mathbb R^2\) is not bounded; \(f=x\) has no maximum.
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Theorem extreme value theorem

      Points on the boundary must be checked as well.

Lagrange multipliers

Problems 37–41
  1. 37

    Use Lagrange multipliers to find the extreme values of \(f(x,y)=3x-2y\) on the circle \(x^2+y^2=4\).

    1. (a)
      The maximum occurs at \(\left(\frac{6}{\sqrt{13}},-\frac{4}{\sqrt{13}}\right)\).
    2. (b)
      At an extreme point of \(f\) on \(g=c\) (with \(\nabla g\neq\mathbf 0\)), \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g\) for some \(\lambda\).
    3. (c)
      The minimum value is \(-2\sqrt{13}\).
    4. (d)
      The maximum value is \(6\), at \((2,0)\).
    5. (e)
      The maximum value is \(2\sqrt{13}\).
    Solution to problem 37

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      The \(+\) sign above.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Theorem method of Lagrange multipliers

      This is the Lagrange condition.
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      The \(-\) sign above.
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(f(2,0)=6\), but \(2\sqrt{13}\approx7.21\) is larger, and \(\nabla f\) is not parallel to \(\nabla g\) at \((2,0)\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      \(\langle3,-2\rangle=\lambda\langle2x,2y\rangle\) means \((x,y)\) is parallel to \(\langle3,-2\rangle\): \((x,y)=\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt{13}}\langle3,-2\rangle\). Then \(f=\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt{13}}\cdot13\).
  2. 38

    Let \(f(x,y)=xy\) subject to the constraint \(5x-y=4\).

    1. (a)
      The maximum value is \(\frac45\).
    2. (b)
      The minimum value is \(-\frac45\).
    3. (c)
      The method of Lagrange multipliers always produces both a maximum and a minimum.
    4. (d)
      The minimum occurs at \(\left(\frac25,-2\right)\).
    5. (e)
      The Lagrange multiplier at the minimum is \(\lambda=2\).
    Solution to problem 38

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(5x^2-4x\) is unbounded above, so there is no maximum.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      Substitute \(y=5x-4\): \(f=5x^2-4x\), minimized at \(x=\frac25\), giving \(\frac45-\frac85\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Theorem method of Lagrange multipliers

      It only finds candidates. Here the constraint is unbounded and \(f\) has no maximum.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(y=5\cdot\frac25-4=-2\).
    5. (e)

      (e) False. Calculation

      \(\langle y,x\rangle=\lambda\langle5,-1\rangle\) gives \(x=-\lambda\); with \(x=\frac25\), \(\lambda=-\frac25\).
  3. 39

    Find the extreme values of \(f(x,y)=x^2y\) on the ellipse \(4x^2+9y^2=36\).

    1. (a)
      The minimum value is \(0\).
    2. (b)
      The maximum occurs at a point with \(x=0\).
    3. (c)
      The maximum value is \(4\sqrt3\).
    4. (d)
      The maximum value is \(6\).
    5. (e)
      The points \((0,\pm2)\) are candidates from the Lagrange equations.
    Solution to problem 39

    Key: two statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) False. Calculation

      \(f=-4\sqrt3\) at \(x^2=6\), \(y=-\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\), which is smaller than \(0\).
    2. (b)

      (b) False. Calculation

      Points with \(x=0\) give \(f=0\). The maximum occurs where \(x^2=6\) and \(y=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(2xy=8\lambda x\), \(x^2=18\lambda y\). If \(x\neq0\): \(y=4\lambda\), \(x^2=72\lambda^2\), and the constraint gives \(\lambda^2=\frac1{12}\). Then \(x^2=6\), \(y=\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\), \(f=\pm\frac{12}{\sqrt3}\).
    4. (d)

      (d) False. Calculation

      \(4\sqrt3\approx6.93\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Theorem method of Lagrange multipliers

      With \(x=0\), the equation \(2xy=8\lambda x\) holds for any \(\lambda\), and \(x^2=18\lambda y\) holds with \(\lambda=0\).
  4. 40

    The following statements concern constrained optimization.

    1. (a)
      With two constraints \(g=c\) and \(h=k\), the Lagrange condition is \(\nabla f=\lambda\nabla g+\mu\nabla h\).
    2. (b)
      The point on \(y=x+\frac1x\), \(x>0\), closest to the origin has \(x=2^{-1/4}\).
    3. (c)
      The maximum of \(f=x-z\) on the intersection of \(x^2+y^2=1\) and \(x^2+z^2=1\) is \(2\).
    4. (d)
      The maximum of \(f=x+2y+3z\) subject to \(x+y+z=1\) and \(x^2+y^2+z^2=1\) is \(2+\frac{2}{\sqrt3}\).
    5. (e)
      The minimum of \(f=x-z\) on the same curve is \(-\sqrt2\).
    Solution to problem 40

    Key: four statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Theorem Lagrange multipliers with two constraints

      At an extreme point, \(\nabla f\) lies in the span of the two constraint gradients.
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      Minimize \(d^2=x^2+\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2=2x^2+2+x^{-2}\): \(4x-2x^{-3}=0\) gives \(x^4=\frac12\).
    3. (c)

      (c) False. Calculation

      \(x^2+z^2=1\) forces \(x-z\le\sqrt2\), attained at \(x=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\), \(z=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\) (with \(y=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\)). The point \(x=1\), \(z=-1\) is not on the cylinder \(x^2+z^2=1\).
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      The constraint set is a circle with center \(\left(\frac13,\frac13,\frac13\right)\) and radius \(\sqrt{2/3}\) in the plane. \(f=2\) at the center, and the component of \(\nabla f\) in the plane is \(\langle-1,0,1\rangle\), of length \(\sqrt2\). So the maximum is \(2+\sqrt2\sqrt{2/3}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      By the same reasoning, attained at \(x=-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\), \(z=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\).
  5. 41

    The following statements concern optimization applications.

    1. (a)
      The largest box with faces parallel to the coordinate planes inscribed in \(\left(\frac xa\right)^2+\left(\frac yb\right)^2+\left(\frac zc\right)^2=1\) has dimensions \(\frac{2a}{\sqrt3}\times\frac{2b}{\sqrt3}\times\frac{2c}{\sqrt3}\).
    2. (b)
      The maximum of \(xyz\) on \(2x+y+4z=1\) with \(x,y,z\ge0\) is \(\frac{1}{216}\), at \(\left(\frac16,\frac13,\frac1{12}\right)\).
    3. (c)
      The minimum of \(x_1^2\sigma_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2\sigma_n^2\) subject to \(x_1+\cdots+x_n=1\) is \(\left(\sum_j\sigma_j^{-2}\right)^{-1}\).
    4. (d)
      That box has volume \(\dfrac{8abc}{3\sqrt3}\).
    5. (e)
      A cylindrical can with a bottom but no top and fixed volume has minimum surface area when its height equals its radius.
    Solution to problem 41

    Key: all five statements are true.

    1. (a)

      (a) True. Calculation

      Maximize \(8xyz\); the Lagrange equations give \(\frac{x^2}{a^2}=\frac{y^2}{b^2}=\frac{z^2}{c^2}=\frac13\).
    2. (b)

      (b) True. Calculation

      \(yz=2\lambda\), \(xz=\lambda\), \(xy=4\lambda\) give \(2x=y=4z\); the constraint gives \(3y=1\).
    3. (c)

      (c) True. Calculation

      \(2x_j\sigma_j^2=\lambda\) gives \(x_j=\frac{\lambda}{2\sigma_j^2}\); the constraint fixes \(\lambda\), and substituting gives the formula.
    4. (d)

      (d) True. Calculation

      \(\frac{2a}{\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{2b}{\sqrt3}\cdot\frac{2c}{\sqrt3}\).
    5. (e)

      (e) True. Calculation

      Minimize \(\pi r^2+2\pi rh\) with \(\pi r^2h=V\): \(\langle2\pi r+2\pi h,\,2\pi r\rangle=\lambda\langle2\pi rh,\,\pi r^2\rangle\) gives \(h=r\).
