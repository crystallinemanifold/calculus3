Why two multipliers

The curve lies on both surfaces, so its tangent T̂ is perpendicular to ∇g and to ∇h. Those two gradients span the whole plane of directions perpendicular to the curve: the gray disk.

Split ∇f into a part in that disk and a part along T̂. The part along T̂ measures how fast f changes as you move along the curve. At a constrained max or min it must vanish, so ∇f lies in the disk: ∇f = λ∇g + μ∇h.

Try this: slide P toward a marked extreme and watch the τT̂ arrow shrink to nothing as the level surface of f becomes tangent to the curve. With hyperboloids, the level surface through P passes from one sheet, through the cone at t = 3π/4, to two sheets.
